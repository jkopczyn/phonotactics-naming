## Core sources (open, downloaded)

### `butskhrikidze2002` — the backbone
**Butskhrikidze, Marika (2002). *The Consonant Phonotactics of Georgian.* PhD dissertation,
Universiteit Leiden. LOT Dissertation Series 63. Utrecht: LOT. ISBN 90-76864-24-1.**
URL: https://www.lotpublications.nl/Documents/063_fulltext.pdf (also mirrored at pure.mpg.de)
Access: **OA** (direct PDF, no login). Status: **confirmed** — exists, is what the baseline
bibliography says it is, 1.04 MB, ~250 pp.
Files: `butskhrikidze2002.pdf`, `butskhrikidze2002.txt` (pdftotext -layout, 10 761 lines).

Covers §2 (primary), §1, §4, §6 in passing. **Chapter map for digesters** (thesis page numbers;
the .txt has no page markers, so navigate by the section headings, which survive extraction):

### `shosted-chikovani2006` — the IPA Illustration
**Shosted, Ryan K. & Chikovani, Vakhtang (2006). "Standard Georgian." *Journal of the
International Phonetic Association* 36(2): 255–264. doi:10.1017/S0025100306002659.**
URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A7DCF9606BA856FCA5CC25918ADB37EF/S0025100306002659a.pdf/standard_georgian.pdf
Access: **OA** — the published PDF is served free from Cambridge Core (verified; the source-plan's
"(verify open access)" is resolved: **yes, open**).
Files: `shosted-chikovani2006.pdf` (published, 10 pp.), `shosted-chikovani2006.txt`.
Also kept: `shosted-chikovani2006-phonlab.pdf/.txt` — the pre-publication version in *UC Berkeley
PhonLab Annual Report* (2006), 13 pp., https://linguistics.berkeley.edu/phonlab/documents/2006/shosted_chikovani_11-23.pdf
(same text, slightly fuller; eScholarship permalink https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6f88p78m).

§1 (28-consonant chart with Georgian letters, 5-vowel system, near-minimal sets), §5 (the
orthographic transcription used alongside IPA), and a transcribed passage (North Wind). Explicitly
notes that consonant clusters and vowel quality in the passage are under-described — do not expect
§2 material here. One speaker (the second author), Tbilisi.

### `crouch2022-diss` — sonority and cluster timing
**Crouch, Caroline (2022). *Postcards from the syllable edge: sonority and articulatory timing in
complex onsets in Georgian.* PhD dissertation, UC Santa Barbara. 160 pp.**
URL: https://escholarship.org/content/qt5w18167d/qt5w18167d_noSplash_a6f766ffc915196c10cf7cb95c999e5b.pdf
Access: **OA** (eScholarship). Status: confirmed. Files: `crouch2022-diss.pdf/.txt`.
§2: complex onsets by sonority profile (rises, plateaus, falls, reversals), which shapes occur and
how they are timed; the "intrusive vocoid" that appears inside low-overlap clusters — relevant to
whether Georgian would repair an Irish cluster at all, and to how output should be romanized.

### `borise-stress` — the stress rule
**Borise, Lena (2023). "Disentangling word stress and phrasal prosody: A view from Georgian."
*Phonological Data and Analysis* 5(1): 1–37. doi:10.3765/pda.v5art1.43. CC BY 3.0.**
URL: https://lnborise.github.io/assets/Borise_PDA_GeorgianStress.pdf (author copy; the Cambridge
Core version is open too). Access: **OA / CC BY**. Files: `borise-stress.pdf/.txt`.
§4: instrumental evidence for **fixed initial stress** (duration-based), plus a low **phrase
accent aligned with the penult** that earlier work mistook for antepenultimate stress. Note the
author PDF carries the journal's unedited template header ("Volume 1, Article 3 (2019)", dummy
author names in the copyright line) — cite the real details above, not the header.

### `jun2007-intonation-wpp` / `jun2007-intonation`
**Jun, Sun-Ah, Vicenik, Chad & Lofstedt, Ingvar (2007). "Intonational phonology of Georgian."
*UCLA Working Papers in Phonetics* 106: 41–57.**
URLs: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8vk1j9n6 (full, 18 pp. → `jun2007-intonation-wpp.pdf/.txt`);
https://sunahjun.humspace.ucla.edu/Workshop2007ICPhS/Jun%20et%20al-Georgian.pdf (2-pp. ICPhS 2007
workshop abstract → `jun2007-intonation.pdf/.txt`). Access: **OA**.
§4: Accentual-Phrase model; and the abstract states the **contested stress positions compactly**
(Robins & Waterson 1952 alternating; Aronson 1990 initial or antepenultimate ≤4 syllables, both in
longer words; their own data: initial). Good raw material for a `CONFLICT:` line in §4.

### `begus-caucasian`
**Beguš, Gašper (2021). "Segmental phonetics and phonology in Caucasian languages." Ch. 15 of
Maria Polinsky (ed.), *The Oxford Handbook of Languages of the Caucasus.* OUP.
doi:10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190690694.013.18 — author's preprint.**
URL: https://gbegus.github.io/assets/pdf/BegusCaucasian.pdf
Access: **OA** (author page; the OUP version is paywalled, the preprint is not). Files:
`begus-caucasian.pdf/.txt`. §1: the three-way laryngeal contrast, ejective phonetics, uvulars,
and how Georgian sits among its neighbours — the cross-check for the PHOIBLE row, and the source
to consult if the strand-4 overlay tempts us toward NW Caucasian extremes.

### `self-syncope` — **found and open** (the baseline's guess was right)
**Self, Stephen (2012?). "An OT analysis of vowel syncope in Georgian." *GIALens* 6(2).
Graduate Institute of Applied Linguistics.**
URL: https://diu.edu/documents/gialens/Vol6-2/Self_Georgian.pdf
Access: **OA** (direct PDF). Status: **confirmed** — the baseline's "(diu.edu)" pointer resolves.
Files: `self-syncope.pdf/.txt`.
Despite the OT framing (ignore per project rules), the descriptive core is directly useful for
§2/§3: **final vowels of morphemes ending in a sonorant delete before vowel-initial suffixes;
the nominative -i and [+high] vowels do NOT syncopate** — this is the mechanism that manufactures
Georgian's giant onset clusters, so it is the rule to mimic if we want Irish input to *gain*
clusters rather than merely keep them. Also gives inventory prose and the classic
consonant-only roots (prc̣kvn- 'skin', gvprc̣kvnis 'he peels us').

## Loanword adaptation (§3 and §7) — the attested-data layer

### `anglicisms-leiden2021` — **the single most valuable §3/§7 source**
**Gabunia, Tekla (2021). *The Pronunciation Variation of Anglicisms in Georgian: The Effects of
Social Factors.* MA thesis (Linguistics — Modern Languages, 60 EC), Leiden University;
supervisor Dick Smakman. 55 pp.**
URL (landing): https://studenttheses.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item:3209478/view — the file
itself is served from a signed Ex Libris URL that the landing page generates in-browser; it is
open (no login), but not fetchable by plain HTTP from the landing URL.
Access: **OA**. Files: `anglicisms-leiden2021.pdf/.txt`.
Covers §3 substitution and §7 attested data:
- **/f/ → [pʰ]** is the Georgian-like adaptation (fricative /f/ is not a Georgian phoneme; ჶ was
  removed from the alphabet); an [f] variant exists as a prestige/English-like pronunciation, so
  the same word has two attested forms.
- **English /p t k/ → Georgian ejectives /pʼ tʼ kʼ/** in the nativized layer, aspirated
  /pʰ tʰ kʰ/ in the English-like layer. This is a real and directly usable substitution rule, and
  it is the one that most obviously supports an ejective-heavy strand-4 output.
- /w/ → /v/ (Georgian-like) or /u/ (English-like); /ŋ/ → /n/ or /ng/; /θ ð/, /j/ discussed.
- **Every borrowed noun takes final -i** — visible in all ~24 items.
- English long vowels shortened; diphthongs discussed; /æ/ → /ɛ/.
- **Appendix B** (p. 51–52 of the PDF) is a table of ~24 English items × 2–4 Georgian
  pronunciation variants, all in IPA — lift these straight into `attested.tsv`.
Caveat for the digest: the thesis's variable is *sociolinguistic*, so each item has a
"Georgian-like / mixed / English-like" set. For a name generator, take the **Georgian-like**
column as the grammar and treat the rest as evidence of how far the prestige layer deviates.

### `georgian-accented-speech`
**"The Most Common Pronunciation Mistakes in Georgian-Accented Speech."** MA thesis, Leiden
University. 76 pp. URL (landing): https://studenttheses.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item:2658680/view
Access: **OA**. Files: `georgian-accented-speech.pdf/.txt`.
The *reverse* direction (Georgian L1 → English L2), which is still evidence about the Georgian

### `kirvalidze2017`

## Reference / orthography / morphology (open, downloaded)

### `wiki-ka` — Georgian language (incl. §Phonology)
**Wikipedia, "Georgian language."** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language
Access: **OA / CC BY-SA**. Files: `wiki-Georgian_language.raw.html`, `wiki-Georgian_language.txt`.
**Correction to the task list: there is no separate en-Wikipedia "Georgian phonology" article** —
`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_phonology` is a redirect to this article's §Phonology.
Covers §1 (consonant table with letters, marginal /f/ and /h/, ejectives, [χʼ] allophone of /qʼ/),
§2 (clusters, harmonic clusters), §4 (stress, "disagreements among linguists"), §6
(morphophonological syncope before -eb-, seven cases, derivation).

### `wiki-ka-romanization`
**Wikipedia, "Romanization of Georgian."** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Georgian
Files: `wiki-Romanization_of_Georgian.raw.html`, `wiki-Romanization_of_Georgian.txt`.
**§5, primary.** Full per-letter table of five systems side by side: national system (2002, State
Department of Geodesy and Cartography + Institute of Linguistics; adopted by BGN/PCGN 2009 and
Google Translate), BGN/PCGN, ISO 9984:1996, ALA-LC, and the "unofficial"/keyboard system. The
national system marks ejectives with an apostrophe (kʼ, pʼ, tʼ, qʼ, tsʼ, chʼ) and uses digraphs
gh, kh, sh, ch, ts, dz, zh — i.e. it is already close to the apostrophe-and-digraph look of
*Kas'queil / Th'tysh*.

### `ungegn-georgian` — the standard behind the national romanization
**UNGEGN Working Group on Romanization Systems, "Report on the current status of United Nations
romanization systems for geographical names — Georgian", version 4.1, December 2018.**
URL: https://arhiiv.eki.ee/wgrs/rom2_ka.htm (HTML) and .../rom2_ka.pdf (2 pp.)
Access: **OA**. Status: **confirmed**, but note the source-plan's `rom1_ka` URL is dead — the
current document is `rom2_ka`. Files: `ungegn-georgian.pdf/.txt`, `ungegn-georgian.html`.
**§5, authoritative.** The 33-letter national romanization table (2002, State Department of
Geodesy and Cartography + Institute of Linguistics, Georgian Academy of Sciences; approved by
presidential decree 24 Feb 2011; adopted by BGN/PCGN 2009), plus an explicit statement of how the
apostrophe's meaning *flipped* between the retired BGN/PCGN 1981 system (apostrophe = aspirate)
and the national system (apostrophe = ejective/abruptive). That flip matters for §5: it means
either convention is attestable for our output, and the modern one puts the apostrophe exactly
where *Th'tysh* and *Kas'queil* put it.

### `wiki-help-ipa-ka`
**Wikipedia, "Help:IPA/Georgian."** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Georgian
Files: `wiki-Help_IPA_Georgian.raw.html`, `wiki-Help_IPA_Georgian.txt`.
§5: IPA ↔ Mkhedruli ↔ romanization ↔ **English approximation** for every segment. This is the
cheapest bridge from the digest's IPA output to a spelling an English reader can attempt.

### `wiki-ka-scripts`
**Wikipedia, "Georgian scripts."** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_scripts
Files: `wiki-Georgian_scripts.raw.html`, `wiki-Georgian_scripts.txt`.
§5 background: letter names, letter order, the archaic letters (ჱ ჲ ჳ ჴ ჵ) and the loan letter
ჶ /f/. Only needed if the digest wants letter-name flavour or archaic letters.

### `wiki-ka-grammar`
**Wikipedia, "Georgian grammar."** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_grammar
Files: `wiki-Georgian_grammar.raw.html`, `wiki-Georgian_grammar.txt`.
**§6, primary open source.** Case endings (nominative **-i** on consonant-final stems, ergative
-ma/-m, genitive -is, adverbial -ad…), the -eb- plural and the syncope it triggers, adjective
agreement, derivational affixes. This is where the "every word ends in -i" fact that most shapes
Georgian-looking output is documented.

### `wiki-ka-name`
**Wikipedia, "Georgian name."** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_name
Files: `wiki-Georgian_name.raw.html`, `wiki-Georgian_name.txt`.
**§6 for epithets/surnames**: the surname suffixes **-dze** 'son', **-shvili** 'child',
**-iani**, **-ani**, **-uri/-uli**, and the regional distribution of each; also the origin suffix
**-eli**. Directly usable as name-forming morphology for the strand.

### `peacecorps-georgian-guide`
**U.S. Peace Corps, *Georgian Language Podcast Reference Guide* (Peace Corps Georgia, n.d.), 23 pp.**
URL: https://www.livelingua.com/peace-corps/Georgian/GE_Georgian_Language_Reference_Guide.pdf
Access: **PD** (U.S. federal government work; hosted free by the Live Lingua Project).
Files: `peacecorps-georgian-guide.pdf`, `peacecorps-georgian-guide.txt`.

Low linguistic depth; use only for §5/§6 texture.

### `brosset1837`
**Brosset, Marie-Félicité (1837). *Éléments de la langue géorgienne.* Paris: Imprimerie Royale.**
URL: https://archive.org/details/GrammarGeorgianBrossetFrench (Google Books scan)

## Datasets produced here

### `ka-wiki-titles` (dataset, not a publication)
`ka-wiki-name-transliterations.tsv` — 118 rows, harvested 2026-08-24 from the en-Wikipedia API
(`action=query&prop=langlinks&lllang=ka`) over a seed list of English/Irish/international personal
names, place names and international loanwords. Two columns: English article title, Georgian
article title (Mkhedruli).
**§7 raw material.** Provenance tag for `attested.tsv`: `ka-wiki-title`. Caveats, which the digest
must apply: (a) these are editorial transliterations, not speech, and many are mediated by Russian
rather than English; (b) some rows are *translations*, not transliterations (e.g. "Alexander the
Great" → ალექსანდრე მაკედონელი) and must be discarded; (c) they do show, in bulk, the two
adaptations that matter most for this project — the **obligatory final -i** on consonant-final
foreign names and the treatment of segments Georgian lacks (/f/, /h/, /θ/, /w/, /æ/).

## Could not find or verify

- **A dedicated, peer-reviewed Georgian loanword-*phonology* paper with a full substitution
  table.** `anglicisms-leiden2021` covers this well enough to work from, but it is an MA thesis and
  its data set is 24 items chosen for sociolinguistic salience, not for phonological coverage.
  Candidates that exist but are login-walled and were therefore abandoned: "The influence of
  English borrowings on the Georgian language" and "Russian and Georgian language contacts"
  (ResearchGate); "On the integration of Anglicisms into present-day Georgian" and "Recently
  borrowed English verbs and their morphological accommodation in Georgian" (academia.edu,
  Cloudflare/login). Lomidze (2008), "Peculiarities of Anglicisms adaptation…" (PhD) exists only
  as a citation — no open full text located.
- **No source found on Russian→Georgian adaptation specifically**, which is a pity: Russian is the
  older and larger contact layer and would give vowel-reduction and palatalization data that the
  English material does not. `asaturova-garibashvili` only gestures at it.
- **georgian.se** ("Kartuli" online grammar, §Phonology at
  https://georgian.se/kartuli/GeoGrammar/PHON/00PHON2.html) looks relevant but the site's web
  application firewall blocks automated requests (HTTP 455). Not retrieved; a human browser could.
- **A Georgian-Wikipedia guideline page on rendering foreign names** — searched ka.wikipedia; no
  "practical transcription" guideline exists (unlike the Russian-Wikipedia model). The
  `ka-wiki-titles` dataset above is the substitute.
