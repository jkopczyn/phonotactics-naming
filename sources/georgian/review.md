# Adversarial review: Georgian digest

## 1. Citation spot-check

**Counts: 30 VERIFIED · 7 MISQUOTED · 0 NOT FOUND · 8 OVERSTATED (45 checks total).**

I checked the rendered PDFs wherever Butskhrikidze's phonetic font mattered. The Appendix 2–3
sample below contains 15 individual cluster checks, in addition to checks of §3.5.1 and the prose

### Inventory, phonotactics, and cluster grammar

1. **VERIFIED — inventory size.** Digest: “28 consonants + 5 vowels” and one letter per phoneme
   [shosted2006 p.255]. Shosted & Chikovani p.255 gives the 28-consonant chart and says Georgian
   orthography is highly transparent, with one symbol corresponding to each phoneme; the five-vowel
   inventory follows on pp.261–262.
2. **VERIFIED — /h/ cannot close a stem.** Digest: “a Georgian stem may end in any of the 27
   consonants ‘except /h/’” [butskhrikidze2002 p.98]. Source p.98: “The stem can end in any of the
   27 consonants (i.e. except /h/).”
3. **VERIFIED — monomorphemic hiatus.** Digest: “two adjacent vowels are disallowed”
   [butskhrikidze2002 p.83]. Source p.83 says exactly that, then identifies loans and compounds as
   exceptions.
4. **VERIFIED — six stem-initial consonants.** Digest: Butskhrikidze's maximum is six, with
   *prckvna* and *brdγvna* [pp.106–108]. Appendix 2 p.205 lists those two six-member sequences.
5. **VERIFIED — harmonic-cluster definition.** Digest: regressive place order plus homogeneous
   laryngeal specification [p.103]. Source p.103: harmonic clusters have “two requirements,
   regressivity and homogeneity”; it defines homogeneity as shared laryngeal specification.
6. **VERIFIED — 32-item harmonic table.** Digest table reproduces source table (53): 16 Type A
   plus 16 Type B entries, including the two nearly unattested symmetry fillers *zg, žg*. Source
   p.103 n.20 explicitly warns that *zg* and *žg* are “almost unattested.”

### Gabunia 2021 and loan adaptation

24. **VERIFIED — overall percentages.** Digest: 59.6% Eng-like and 36.9% Geo-like across 1320
    tokens. Gabunia pp.24–25, Table 3.1: 788/1320 = 59.6% and 488/1320 = 36.9%.
25. **VERIFIED — context percentages.** Digest's 43.7%, 84.1%, 26.5%, and 43.4% Geo-like rates
    reproduce Table 3.2 on p.25.
26. **OVERSTATED — categorical positional rule.** Digest turns the table into “After a consonant,
    ejective; between vowels, aspirate.” Gabunia says only that Geo-like pronunciation was
    *preferred* after a consonant (84.1%), gives 26.5% Geo-like even intervocalically, and warns on
    p.26 that “the data is still too small to make any assumptions” and the findings are
    “preliminary ... merely presenting the tendencies.” This can be a weighted tendency or a
    design choice, not an attested categorical grammar rule.
27. **OVERSTATED — “nativized” versus “prestige” layers.** Gabunia's terms are **Geo-like** and
    **Eng-like**; p.19 says Eng-like is “in fact an in-between variant.” She does not call the
    latter a prestige grammar. Her social results support an association with youth, English
    command, and technological advancement, but the digest silently converts a sociolinguistic
    continuum and a prescriptive transliteration norm into two phonological layers.
28. **VERIFIED — within-item consistency as an observation.** Digest quotes p.26: participants
    used either English-like or Georgian-like plosives in both positions in *casting, Twitter,
    speaker*. That quotation is accurate. The further implementation instruction to make it
    unrestricted “word-level harmony” is broader than the three reported items, but the cited
    observation itself is present.
29. **OVERSTATED — labial “blocks” ejectivity.** Digest: “Plosive + labial blocks the ejective.”
    Gabunia p.26 says *Queen* had no ejective tokens and that “most other loanwords” of this type
    use aspirates, then immediately names old-loan exceptions. This is a tendency, not a block.
30. **OVERSTATED — “Georgian barely repairs anything.”** The cited loan corpus contains many
    unrepaired clusters, including *inst'agrami, bit'lzi, p'azli,* and *rekordsmeni*. That verifies
    the examples, not the language-wide claim that “across all four loanword sources” Georgian
    performs no cluster repair or leaves Irish “almost untouched.” Gabunia's 24 experimental items
    were selected for variation in /p t k w f eɪ/, not to test illicit clusters; p.18 lists those
    variables, and p.26 stresses the small, preliminary dataset. Absence of a discussion in this
    corpus is not evidence that every Irish cluster is admitted or that repair never occurs.
31. **VERIFIED — loss of vowel length.** Gabunia p.13: “all Georgian vowels are short,” and English
    long/tense vowels are replaced by corresponding short vowels.
32. **VERIFIED — nominative -i in the experiment.** Gabunia p.19 says there was practically no
    fully English-like pronunciation because all loanwords take Georgian suffix *-i*; p.12 calls
    it the nominative marker.

### Romanization and the existing names

33. **VERIFIED — apostrophe in the national 2002 system.** Digest: apostrophe marks ejectives.
    UNGEGN p.1 maps კ→`k'`, პ→`p'`, ტ→`t'`, ყ→`q'`, წ→`ts'`, ჭ→`ch'`, and says the apostrophe
    signifies an “abruptive sound.”
34. **VERIFIED — retired BGN/PCGN inversion.** UNGEGN p.1 says the retired system put apostrophes
    on aspirates and left abruptives unmarked; the digest reproduces the contrast correctly.
35. **OVERSTATED — *Xelxyx* is not “phonologically perfect apart from y.”** /xɛlxix/ is a possible
    **stem**, but the digest's own cited source says a well-formed minimal word is vowel-final and
    cannot end in a consonant [butskhrikidze2002 p.98]. This assessment silently assumes the later,
    disputed bare-stem output policy.
36. **MISQUOTED — *Ysclyth* is not read “under national 2002” as /isklitʰ/.** The national table
    has separate `s`, `k`, `l`, `i`, `t`; it has no `sc` digraph and no `y` vowel. The proposed
    pronunciation is a project-specific reinterpretation, not a reading under the cited system.
37. **MISQUOTED — *Kas'queil* reverses standard apostrophe placement.** Digest says the apostrophe
    after `s` can “belong to the following” `q`. UNGEGN's mappings are consistently consonant **then**
    apostrophe (`q'`), so `s'q` is not `sq'`. The digest correctly senses that the name does not
    parse, but its proposed parse is contrary to the standard it cites.
38. **MISQUOTED — *Th'tysh* explanation reverses the same convention.** Digest says “there is no
    consonant after the apostrophe for it to ejectivize.” In the national system the ejectivized
    consonant is **before** the apostrophe. The real problems are that `th` is not a national
    digraph and that `y` is not a Georgian vowel.
39. **MISQUOTED — *Tchaeul* does not “match” Peace Corps `t'ch`.** `tch` lacks the apostrophe in
    `t'ch`; under the national system `ch'` is ejective while `ch` is aspirated. In addition, §2.9
    itself documents loanword hiatus, so the claim that `aeu` is simply a “hard violation” needs
    the narrower qualification “not native monomorphemic phonotactics.”

### Morphology, provenance, stress, and Irish mappings

40. **VERIFIED — consonant-stem nominative -i.** `wiki-ka-grammar §Declension` gives *k'ats-i*
    beside *k'ats-ma, k'ats-is,* etc. The digest correctly identifies *-i* as a case suffix.
41. **OVERSTATED — a bare stem is not itself a word-form.** Digest: “*k'ats-* is as much a Georgian
    word-form as *k'atsi* is.” The cited declension table labels *k'ats-* the root/stem and shows it
    only with case endings. The source supports outputting a morphological stem as a deliberate
    project convention; it does not support calling that stem an independently usable Georgian
    word-form.
42. **VERIFIED — Wikipedia provenance disclosure.** `bib.md` defines `ka-wiki-title` as editorial
    transliteration, not speech; all 88 such rows in `attested.tsv` say “target_ipa derived from
    Georgian orthography.” The digest's §7 disclosure is accurate.
43. **MISQUOTED — *Connacht* derived IPA.** The row gives target form **კონაქტი** but target IPA
    **/kʼɔnɑkʰtʼi/**. Georgian კ is /kʼ/, not /kʰ/; the mechanically derived form is
    **/kʼɔnɑkʼtʼi/**. Consequently §8.4's claimed precedent `/xt/ → /kʰtʼ/` is also wrong: this
    editorial spelling shows `/xt/ → /kʼtʼ/` if one infers a segment mapping at all.
44. **VERIFIED — fixed initial stress as Borise's conclusion.** Borise p.32 concludes that initial
    duration is best interpreted as fixed initial stress. The digest also preserves the contrary
    literature under a `CONFLICT:` line.
45. **OVERSTATED — Irish /h/ “delete elsewhere.”** The cited native restriction says /h/ never
    occurs in native consonant sequences, but the digest's own editorial loan evidence includes
    *Stockholm* **სტოკჰოლმი** /stʼɔkʼhɔlmi/, a /kʼh/ sequence. One deletion in *Tehran* does not
    establish a general loan rule. The rule conflicts with the data and should remain unresolved.

## 2. Uncited or under-cited claims

1. **The categorical repair headline is under-supported.** “Georgian barely repairs anything,”
   “will leave Irish input almost untouched,” and “no repair” generalize from a convenience sample
   that was not designed around cluster phonotactics. These require narrowing to “no cluster repair
   occurs in the collected examples” and an explicit coverage warning.
2. **The `/p t k/` grammar is not cleanly a nativized/prestige split.** “Take the Geo-like column
   as the grammar” is a project decision, not a result of Gabunia, and should be marked as such.
3. **The positional `/w/` rule is a synthesis, not an attested rule.** The digest does call it “this
   digest's reading,” but the rule itself lacks `(unattested)` and conflates direct English /w/ with
   Russian/German orthographic W pronounced /v/.
4. **Several vowel rules are inferred without the template's `(unattested)` tag:** `/æ/ → /ɛ~ɑ/`,
   `/ʌ/ → /ɑ/`, and the Irish diphthong mappings `/iə uə əi əu/ → /iɑ uɑ ɛi ɔu/`.
5. **The five-name readings are project inventions.** `y = i`, `sc = sk`, `tch = tʃʼ`, moving an
   apostrophe from before `q` to after it, and interpreting `ue` as /vɛ/ or hiatus are not readings
   licensed by the national system. They need `(unattested)`/“proposed respelling,” not presentation
   as standard readings.
6. **D3 `y` and D4 bare-stem output are overlays.** Both are defensible design proposals, but D3
   has no Georgian source and D4 produces a morphological representation rather than an ordinary
   nominative word. The current wording understates both departures.
7. **The `/h/ → ∅` environment is inferred from one editorial title.** There is contrary editorial
   evidence in the same dataset.
8. **`/stʰ/ → /stʼ/` is based on editorial spellings, often Russian-mediated.** It is useful as a
   deterministic convention but should not be called “the single most reliable repair Georgian
   applies to English input” without distinguishing transliteration from speech.
9. **Wikipedia rows are all tagged `source_lang=eng`, even when the notes assert a different route**
   (Greek/Russian *Athens*, Russian *Baku*, German *München*, Russian *Stambul*, etc.). English is
   the API article-title language, not necessarily the donor language. The donor field should be
   corrected or explicitly defined as “English editorial source string,” while mediation remains
   unknown/mixed.
10. **The Gabunia `target_form` spellings are derived but not tagged as derived.** Appendix B gives
    English item labels and Georgian IPA variants, not the Mkhedruli spellings found in
    `attested.tsv`. Unlike the Wikipedia IPA derivations, these reconstructed orthographic forms are
    not disclosed in `provenance` or `note`, contrary to `ATTESTED-FORMAT.md`'s “as given” rule.

## 3. Rule/example consistency

### Rules lacking an attested instantiation

- **/ð/ → /t, d, z/** has no `attested.tsv` row and no concrete loan example. It is only a prose
  possibility in Gabunia p.14.
- **Direct-perception /θ/ → /s/** has no Georgian loan row; the only `/θ/` item, *thriller*, is
  explicitly Russian-mediated and uses aspirated /tʰ/.
- **General `/h/ → ∅` outside initial/intervocalic position** has only *Tehran* and is contradicted
  by *Stockholm*.
- **Final devoicing in adapted loans** has no instantiation; §3.5 correctly says *-i* normally
  bleeds it, so it should not be emitted as a productive loan rule.
- **Categorical word-level laryngeal harmony** is instantiated only by the three experimental
  items named by Gabunia. There is no evidence that it extends to every multi-stop Irish input,
  especially across mixed source segments and compounds.
- **The recommended Irish diphthong mappings and schwa fallback** have no attested rows.

The remaining §3 processes do have examples: ejective and aspirate stop variants, /f/→/pʰ/,
/w/→/u~v/, /ŋ/→/nɡ/, /j/→/i/ or revived ჲ, length loss, diphthong monophthongization/hiatus,
nominative *-i*, and degemination.

## 4. Internal contradictions and missing CONFLICT lines

1. **Native /h/ restriction versus loan /h/.** §2.8 says /h/ “never occurs in consonant sequences”;
   §8.2 converts that into deletion outside initial/intervocalic position; yet §7/`attested.tsv`
   includes *Stockholm* /stʼɔkʼhɔlmi/. Add a `CONFLICT:` between native-stem phonotactics and loan
   orthography; do not choose deletion from one *Tehran* title.
2. **No cluster repair versus attested process labels.** §3 says final *-i* is morphological, but
   almost every consonant-final row labels it `epenthesis`. The rule writer cannot tell phonological
   epenthesis from case suffixation.
3. **National-system assessment versus proposed orthography.** §5.2 says it reads names “under
   national 2002,” but all five readings rely on non-national conventions. Separate “literal
   national parse” (mostly impossible) from “proposed strand-compatible respelling.”
4. **Apostrophe direction contradicts itself.** §5.1 correctly says national apostrophe follows
   and marks the ejective consonant; §5.2 then makes the apostrophe in *Kas'queil* attach forward
   and says *Th'tysh* lacks a following consonant. Both statements reverse §5.1.
5. **Hiatus is both tolerated and a hard violation.** §2.9 correctly says loans are precisely the
   environment where VV occurs; §5.2 calls *Tchaeul*'s vowel sequence a hard Georgian violation
   without limiting the statement to native monomorphemic vocabulary.
6. **Bare stem as output versus Georgian word phonotactics.** §2.8/§8.6 correctly state that a
   well-formed minimal word is vowel-final; §6.1 calls a bound stem “as much a Georgian word-form”
   as the nominative. The design can output stems, but it must be presented as a deliberate
   non-citation-form convention, not a resolution of the linguistic conflict.
7. **“Every consonant-final foreign name takes -i” versus bare non-final elements and given names.**
   The digest later supplies *jorj, elvis, maik'l* and consonant-final Georgian personal names.
   The scope must be narrowed to the right edge of the inflected name phrase/common noun, and the
   special behavior of personal names needs a source or a `CONFLICT:`.
8. **“Whole cluster grammar” versus listed exceptions.** §2.4 recommends the six-slot template as
   the whole licitness test, while §2.3/§2.6 list /s/+obstruent, /r/+obstruent, loan clusters, and
   other CC sequences outside that regressive homogeneous template. The template is a generator of
   canonical long clusters, not a complete acceptance test.
9. **`/st/` is called cluster repair although only laryngeal substitution occurs.** The cluster is
   retained; `/tʰ/→/tʼ/` is the §3.1 stop-series choice. Encoding a separate `/st/→/stʼ/` repair
   would duplicate and potentially conflict with the general stop rule.
10. **Missing `CONFLICT:` for Gabunia's scope warning.** The digest reports her preliminary warning
    but still chooses categorical rules from the same table. The uncertainty belongs at the rule,
    not only in prose later.

## 5. Fitness for purpose

1. **Do not use the Appendix lists as currently transcribed.** At minimum restore `t'χ'n`, correct
   `č'k'n` to `c'k'n`, normalize all bare `j` glyphs, and change the final-CCCC summary from a
   categorical pattern to a tendency. A deterministic whitelist with one affricate place changed
   will reject valid Georgian clusters and admit unattested ones.
2. **Separate three objects that are currently conflated:** native stem phonotactics, ordinary
   inflected word forms, and editorial foreign-name transliteration. They have different edge and
   /h/ behavior. One combined licitness test will be wrong.
3. **The six-slot template cannot be the whole cluster acceptor.** Use it to generate canonical
   long regressive clusters, but preserve explicit whitelists/classes for /s/+C, /r/+C, loans, and
   stem-final sequences.
4. **The stop-series decision needs a deterministic policy labelled as a design choice.** Gabunia
   supports ejective as the official Geo-like norm and as an 84.1% post-consonantal tendency; she
   does not support a categorical environment rule. A rule writer needs either (a) official norm
   everywhere with listed lexical exceptions, or (b) a stated project overlay. Calling measured
   Eng-like output “prestige” does not resolve the choice.
5. **Cluster non-repair needs a bounded statement.** Safe implementation: preserve a source
   cluster if it passes the native/loan licitness test; otherwise leave the repair unresolved.
   Unsafe implementation: pass every Irish cluster because no repair happened in 24 selected
   anglicisms.
6. **Romanization needs literal parses before compatibility proposals.** The five existing names
   are poor matches to national Georgian romanization: none is fully parseable; only *Xelxyx* is
   close after adopting nonstandard `x` and `y`; *Ysclyth* needs `sc` and `y`; *Kas'queil* has the
   apostrophe on the wrong side of `q`; *Th'tysh* has nonstandard `th` and `y`; *Tchaeul* lacks the
   apostrophe required for the proposed ejective reading and has an exceptional vowel run. The
   current verdicts make the fit look substantially better than it is.
7. **Fix attested data before deriving rules from it.** Correct *Connacht*, normalize O'Connor's IPA
   boundary, disclose Gabunia-derived orthography, and stop treating `source_lang=eng` as a known
   historical donor for every Wikipedia title.
8. **Do not implement `/h/` deletion or `/xt/→/kʰtʼ/` from the present digest.** The former is
   contradicted by *Stockholm*; the latter is based on a letter-to-IPA error and should be
   `/kʼtʼ/` for the actual spelling *konakt'i*.
9. **Keep morphology out of phonological epenthesis.** Represent nominative *-i* as a suffix or a
   separate output-mode step. This is especially important because the strand may intentionally
   output bare stems.
10. **The sources could have supplied a safer negative result:** there is strong evidence that
    many large clusters are retained and that intrusive vocoids are not phonological vowels. That
    supports “do not automatically insert a written vowel into a licit cluster,” not “repair
    nothing.”

## 6. Verdict

### Required fixes before use

- Correct the Appendix transcription errors (`t'χ'n`, `c'k'n`, bare-`j` normalization) and the
  overstated final-CCCC pattern; do not treat the six-slot template as the complete cluster grammar.
- Rewrite §3's headline and rules to distinguish **observed non-repair in this dataset** from a
  language-wide “repairs nothing” rule. Preserve Gabunia's percentages but label the environmental
  effects as preliminary tendencies, not categorical conditioning.
- Replace the “nativized vs prestige” binary with Gabunia's actual Geo-like/Eng-like labels plus an
  explicit project decision about which deterministic layer to implement.
- Correct §5's five-name assessment: the 2002 national system does put apostrophes **after
  ejectives**, but the existing names do not follow it consistently. Fix the backwards analyses of
  *Kas'queil* and *Th'tysh* and the false `tch`≈`t'ch` equivalence.
- Correct `attested.tsv`-derived claims in the digest: *Connacht* is /kʼɔnɑkʼtʼi/, not
  /kʼɔnɑkʰtʼi/; no `/xt/→/kʰtʼ/` precedent follows. Treat *Stockholm* as a conflict for the proposed
  /h/-deletion rule.
- Before using `attested.tsv` as test data, disclose derived Gabunia spellings, correct the two
  sampled Wikipedia IPA defects, and distinguish editorial title language from historical donor
  language. Keep nominative *-i* morphologically separate from phonological epenthesis.
- Mark the bare-stem, `y=i`, positional `/w/`, Irish diphthong, schwa, and palatalization policies as
  explicit project overlays/decisions rather than sourced Georgian rules.
