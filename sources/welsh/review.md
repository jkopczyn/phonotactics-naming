# Adversarial review: Welsh digest

## 1. Citation spot-check

**Counts:** 35 claims checked: **27 VERIFIED, 2 MISQUOTED, 1 NOT FOUND, 5 OVERSTATED**.

1. **VERIFIED — §0, Southern stop inventory.** Digest: “`williams1995`, the Southern source, writes the same contrast as p/b, t/d, k/g.” On the actual PDF page, Table 1 has Welsh `p b`, `t d`, `k g`, and the prose says, “The accent modelled was a South Welsh one.” The PDF, not the destroyed text extraction, supports the digest.
2. **VERIFIED — §1, restricted labialised consonants.** Digest: “`/lʷ nʷ rʷ/` — ‘three used in restricted contexts’.” The actual `williams1995` PDF p.1 says the phoneme set included “three used in restricted contexts (`/lʷ, nʷ, rʷ/`)”; all three appear in Table 1.
3. **VERIFIED — §1, 19 Southern vowels and English-only extras.** Digest: “51 segments, split between 32 consonants and 19 vowels … `/ʒ/, /ɔː/, /ɜː/` purely English.” The actual PDF p.1 says exactly “51 segments, split between 32 consonants and 19 vowels” and names the three English extras. Table 2 visually contains the 19 Welsh vowel entries; this cannot safely be checked from `pdftotext`.
4. **VERIFIED — §1, marginal Southern `/z/`.** Digest: “marginally found in some English loanwords in South Welsh … frequently adapted to fortis `/s/`.” Breit says: “Alveolar lenis `/z/` is marginally found in some English loanwords in South Welsh … though even in common English loans it is frequently adapted to fortis `/s/`” [breit2019 p.62].
5. **VERIFIED — §1, Southern affricates.** Digest: “`/tʃ/, /dʒ/` … established in some dialects, especially in South Welsh … `/t, d/` preceding `/i/`.” Breit gives that statement and *'sgidiau* as the example [breit2019 p.62].
6. **VERIFIED — §1, dorsal-fricative conflict.** Digest preserves both “usually `/x/` in South Welsh and `/χ/` in North Welsh” and “Most dialects have uvular `[χ]`, but some South Welsh dialects have `[x]`.” Both statements occur on consecutive pages of Breit [pp.61–62], so the conflict is real rather than an extraction error.
7. **VERIFIED — §1, Southern `[ʍ]`.** Digest: “Initial `/χw/` is colloquially realised as `[ʍ]` in the south.” The source says exactly this and gives *chwech* `/χweːχ/` → `[ʍeːχ]` [wiki-cy-phon §Consonants].
8. **VERIFIED — §1, Southern light `/l/`.** Digest: “Southern Welsh `/l/` is light … in onset and coda.” The JIPA source says: “Northern Welsh `/l/` is described as dark … while Southern Welsh `/l/` is light (non-velarized `[l]`) in onset and coda positions” [jipa-north pp.494–495]. This is explicitly a literature statement about the South inside a Northern illustration.
9. **MISQUOTED — §2.2, `/fr/` labelled STATED.** Digest acknowledges Breit’s printed “fricative-liquid (`[fl, pr]`)” is a probable typo and silently changes the second member to `/fr/`, then puts `/fr/` in the **STATED** table. The PDF and text both really say `[fl, pr]` [breit2019 p.252]. `/fr/` may be the intended form and is lexically plausible, but it is not stated by this citation. It belongs under ASSEMBLED/typo-conjecture unless separately attested.
10. **VERIFIED — §2.2, the other cluster classes.** Digest quotes `/tl, pr/`, `/sl, sr/`, `/st, sp, sk/`, and `/stl, spr, skl/`. Breit’s source line gives exactly those members [p.252].
11. **OVERSTATED — §2.2, `/dl/` presented as a STATED licit onset.** Digest: “`/dl/` — *dlos* `[dɫoːs]` ‘pretty’.” The cited source supplies the single lexical form and says `[ɫ]` after `/d/` is a Southern exception; it does not state a productive `/dl/` onset class. The token verifies that `/dl/` occurs, but the **STATED** label overstates the source’s generality.
12. **VERIFIED — §2.2, lexical `/bl, gl/`.** Digest: “`/bl/` *blith*, `/gl/` *glo*.” Wood explicitly lists `/#ClV/` with *blith* and *glo* and a voiced lateral [wood1988 p.230]. The digest correctly calls these “lexically” attested rather than a complete class statement.
13. **VERIFIED — §2.2, branching-onset constraints.** Digest says mismatched voicing is impossible, fricative+fricative is illicit, and a fricative cannot occupy the right-hand branch. Breit says “`*[dʃ]` is not a possible onset cluster because such clusters must agree in voicing,” “`*[θʃ]`, being a fricative–fricative sequence, is likewise illicit,” and “fricatives are not permitted in the right-hand portion of Welsh branching onsets” [pp.66–67].
14. **VERIFIED — §2.1, Northern CCC onset and coda maxima.** Digest: “up to three consonants in the onset” and “codas … up to three consonants … `/s/ + /t/ + liquid` (*meistr*).” The JIPA source states both verbatim [pp.505–506]. The digest correctly tags them North.
15. **NOT FOUND — §2.3, assembled `/vl/` coda from *gwefl*.** Digest includes “`/vl/` *gwefl*” in an ASSEMBLED list, but *gwefl* is absent from every held Welsh source text, and no citation is attached to that member. The generic coda templates do not establish this particular cluster. It may be a real Welsh word, but it is not verified by this corpus.
16. **OVERSTATED — §2.1, “Southern surface maximum is very likely CC.”** `williams1994` states epenthesis in a defined set of final C+`l/n/r` sequences; it does not state a Southern maximum coda, and it does not rule out every Southern CCC coda. The digest turns a repair in one cluster class into a global maximum. This should be `(unattested inference)` or removed.
17. **VERIFIED — §2.3/§3.2, Southern final epenthesis rule.** Digest gives C1 `{b d g p t k f v ð s χ θ}` before C2 `{l n r}`, plus `/ml/`, with copy-vowel quality. Williams says: “an epenthetic vowel occurs after one of `/b, d, g, p, t, k, f, v, dh, s, x, th/` when followed by one of `/l, n, r/`, or between `/m/` and `/l/`, at the end of a word,” and “The vowel to be inserted is identical to that of the preceding syllable”; after a diphthong it copies the second element [williams1994 §2.1]. *aml*, *llwybr*, and *cefn* are also present.
18. **VERIFIED — §2.4, word-internal coda maximum.** Digest says word-internal codas contain at most one consonant. Breit & Harris say: “Word-internally, codas contain at most only one consonant” and contrast final *plant, gardd* with medial *plentyn, gerddi* [breit-harris2014 p.1].
19. **VERIFIED — §8.6, broad native s+C prothesis statement.** Digest: “Initial s followed by a consonant has developed a prosthetic y-.” Morris Jones says exactly that, not merely s+stop, and adds that in speech it “is not heard except in words in which it is accented” [morrisjones1913 §23 ii p.26].
20. **VERIFIED — §3.1, historical loan s+stop prothesis and colloquial non-pronunciation.** Parry §84 is located by section number in the OCR and says “W developed an on-glide before s + stop initially,” then: “In colloquial speech, however, this prosthetic y is hardly ever pronounced unless the accent falls on it.” The listed *ysgarlat, ystiwart, ysturmant, ystondardd* examples are present [p.223 §84].
21. **VERIFIED — §3.1, historical `/kn-/` retention.** Parry §80 says: “There is no trace in W borrowings of a t sound” and heads the list “Examples of E kn- retained as cn- in W,” including *cnaf, cnap, cnoc, cnot, cnwpa* [pp.220–221 §80]. This verifies the historical examples. It is still historical evidence, not by itself a modern productivity experiment.
22. **VERIFIED — §3.1, `/kw-/` ~ `/χw-/` historical split.** Parry §82 says initial *qu* “has been retained as cw in W in some cases, in others it has become chw,” and proposes that “initial cw- is foreign to W … whereas initial chw is common.” Both *cwarel* and *chwarel* are listed [pp.221–222 §82].
23. **VERIFIED — §3.2, the modern *pobl / ffenestr / ewythr* triad.** The source says final stop/fricative + `/l,r/` tends to be removed; gives copy epenthesis (*pobl, cancr, brwydr*), deletion where epenthesis would create antepenultimate stress (*ffenestr, posibl*), and occasional `/θr/` metathesis (*ewythr*) [wiki-cy-phon §Other consonants].
24. **VERIFIED — §3.3, historical final stop voicing.** Parry §113 states the two environments exactly: after a long vowel in a monosyllable and after a vowel in a polysyllable; it contrasts retention after a short vowel in a monosyllable and calls the voicing “a truly phonetic change” [pp.239–240 §§113–114]. The digest’s ME/early-NE tag is honest here.
25. **VERIFIED — §3.3/§8.7, modern final loan stops and retained length.** Wood says modern Welsh final `/p t k/` occur in modern loans and lists *map, siop, mat, cloc* with long/geminate final consonants; then: “For loanwords where an original long vowel has been retained, the final consonant is usually lenited,” with *clog* ‘cloak’ [wood1988 pp.234–235].
26. **VERIFIED — §3.4, historical `/tʃ dʒ/` loss of the stop.** Parry §87 says English initial `ch (= tsh)` is “generally represented in W by si” and “There is apparently no trace of the dental stop”; §88 says of `/dʒ/` loans, “there is no trace of the dental” except one *dsiet* [pp.225–227]. The digest correctly tags the substitution table as historical.
27. **VERIFIED — §3.4, modern `/dʒ/` retention.** The orthography source lists modern spellings with ⟨j⟩, and the phonology/orthography material gives *jeli* `/ˈdʒɛli/`; the digest’s modern row is supported [wiki-cy-orth §Non-native letters; §Letter names].
28. **OVERSTATED — §8.1, “every foreign palatal collapses onto `/ʃ/`.”** The evidence immediately cited is Parry’s historical treatment of English `/tʃ dʒ/`; the digest itself elsewhere records modern `/tʃ dʒ/` retention and historical final `/dʒ/` outcomes `/s, ts, ds/`. “Every” and the present-tense framing erase both period and positional exceptions. This needs to read “in the ME/early-NE layer, initial `/tʃ dʒ/` generally …”.
29. **VERIFIED — §8.2, historical Welsh `/ɣ/` loss, correctly identified as sound change.** Morris Jones says “The voiced spirant [ɣ] had then disappeared” by Middle Welsh [§19 iv p.23]; later, “The soft mutation of g has uniformly disappeared as an initial sound,” while medially it disappears or becomes `/j/`, and final *daly/boly* yield Southern *dala/bola* [§110 ii]. The digest explicitly warns that this is Old-to-Middle-Welsh sound change, not loan adaptation.
30. **VERIFIED — §8.4, `/h/` distribution and south-east loss.** The source says `/h/` is normally initial or begins a stressed syllable, disappears after stress, is never final, and disappears in all positions in parts of south-east Wales together with replacement of `/r̥/` by `/r/` [wiki-cy-phon §Fricatives].
31. **VERIFIED — §8.5, historical initial `/l, r/` fortition with later retention.** Parry §95 says initial `l` and `r` “were changed … very often into ll … and rh,” but “The later borrowings naturally tend to preserve the l and r unchanged,” and supplies examples on both sides [p.231 §95]. The digest’s optional/historical reading is fair.
32. **OVERSTATED — §8.7, a general Irish rule inferred from one modern loan pattern.** Digest: “So: an Irish long vowel in a final stressed syllable is preserved by voicing/leniting the following consonant … the one clean, attested answer.” Wood’s wording is weaker: “For loanwords where an original long vowel has been retained, the final consonant is usually lenited,” followed by one example, *clog* ‘cloak’ [p.235]. This is good precedent, but not a categorical Irish→Welsh rule; “usually” and the limited evidence must survive into the rule file.
33. **MISQUOTED — §8.9, donor-side unlenition.** Digest: “Loans enter as unmutated radicals … **So an Irish lenited form should be un-lenited before adaptation**.” Wood says the Welsh loan is accommodated into the **Welsh mutation system** by being assigned an unmutated Welsh radical [p.231]. That does not say to reverse morphological alternations in the donor language before phonological adaptation. The proposed Irish preprocessing rule is not licensed by the citation and must be marked a source-side design decision, not a Welsh adaptation fact.
34. **VERIFIED — §6/§8.9, personal names and mutation.** The spoken-corpus source says: “In the corpus, the names of individuals do not mutate and this is viewed as zero mutation,” while “the names of places can be mutated” [bmj-mutations p.21]. The digest accurately preserves the exceptions for imaginary characters/brands.
35. **OVERSTATED — §9.1, Llanwrtyd inferred from Powyseg/Powys.** Breit says the traditional dialect **Y Bowyseg** is grouped with North Welsh, though often broadly called Mid Welsh [pp.53–54]. Breit does not mention Llanwrtyd, the PHOIBLE survey, or administrative Powys, and does not establish that a locality in modern Powys speaks the traditional Powyseg dialect. The safe claim is “Breit groups Y Bowyseg with North; whether Llanwrtyd belongs to that dialect is unverified.” The digest’s current “Llanwrtyd is in Powys, and Breit groups Powyseg with North” invites an unsupported administrative-area ⇒ dialect inference.

## 2. Uncited or under-cited claims

1. **`/fr/` is not STATED by breit2019.** The typo correction from printed `[pr]` to intended `[fr]` is an inference and needs ASSEMBLED/typo-conjecture status.
2. **The “STATED onsets” table mixes three evidence types:** explicit class membership (`/tl, pr, sl, sr, st, sp, sk, stl, spr, skl/`), a single lexical token (`/dl/`), and descriptive sequences whose syllabic analysis is disputed (`gw-`, `χw-`). Split those types rather than putting them under one load-bearing tag.
3. **The ASSEMBLED coda list is not member-cited.** In particular `/vl/` *gwefl* is not in the held corpus. `/lt/` and `/ɬt/` are both assigned *gwallt*, but orthographic ⟨ll⟩ supports `/ɬt/`, not `/lt/`. Each member needs an actual cited word and IPA, or removal.
4. **“Southern surface maximum is very likely CC”** is a global inference from one epenthesis class, without `(unattested)`.
5. **“ASSEMBLED generalisation: Southern word-final CC is restricted to falling or level sonority”** is plausible but stronger than Williams’s enumerated C+sonorant rule; it does not demonstrate all possible rising-sonority types or all falling/level types. Mark it as an inference, not as a closed phonotactic rule.
6. **§8.6’s “all permitted” list** (`/bl br gl gr dr tr kr kl fl fr/`) uses `[morrisjones1913 passim]` and broad section ranges rather than a citation per lexical form. This is inadequate for a list intended to become code.
7. **`/gn/` from *gnawd*** is cited only as “[morrisjones1913, Pr.Kelt. *gnāto-*]”, without page or section.
8. **The stress interpretation of s+C prothesis** — “for a two-syllable Irish input … stress lands on the epenthetic vowel” — is the digest author’s derivation, not a source statement. It is identified as a “Reading” and “decision point,” but should still carry `(unattested inference)` because it is close to executable rule text.
9. **The §8.3 conclusion “Either way no repair is needed”** is a design inference from Welsh inventory fit, not observed Irish `/x/` adaptation. The §8 header warns generally, but this sentence is categorical enough to need an explicit design label.
10. **The §8.9 donor-side unlenition rule** is not merely under-cited; the cited Welsh-radical evidence does not entail it at all.
11. **Historical processes in §3.6 are not individually period-tagged.** Metathesis, `/r…r/` dissimilation, medial provection, and the front-glide rule are all from Parry’s historical/dialectal corpus. The §3 opening warning helps, but a rule writer extracting bullets can easily miss it. Each Parry-derived bullet should say `(ME/early NE)` or its actual late/dialectal period.
12. **§8.1’s palatal discussion lacks period tags at the claim level.** Its strongest wording is present-tense and universal even though the supporting rules are historical and conflict with the modern affricate layer.
13. **The Romanization table is only globally cited.** Load-bearing rows such as `/tʃ/ → ⟨ts⟩ (also ti-)`, `/ʃ/ → ⟨si⟩`, and `/z/ → ⟨s⟩` should each retain a source/period note, because the historical and modern conventions differ.

## 3. Rule/example consistency

### §3 rule instantiation

Using the review brief’s standard of an `attested.csv` row **or an in-text example**, **no §3 rule lacks an instantiation**. The major rule families all have examples:

- s+stop prothesis: *scarlet → ysgarlat*, *steward → ystiwart*, *instrument → ysturmant*;
- `/kn-/` retention: *knave → cnaf*, *knock → cnoc*, *knot → cnot*;
- `/kw-/ ~ /χw-/`: *quarrel → cwarel/chwarel*;
- final C+sonorant repair: *pobl*, *ffenestr*, *ewythr*, plus *aml, llwybr, cefn*;
- historical final voicing/devoicing/spirantisation/lateral and nasal repairs: many Parry rows;
- modern final gemination/lenition: *map, siop, mat, cloc, clog*;
- absent-segment substitutions: each table row has in-text examples and nearly all have TSV rows;
- vowel adaptation and stress classes: multiple Parry and Buczek rows.

This is not the same as saying the rules are safe: several are historical, optional, dialectal, or inferred from one example.

## 4. Internal contradictions and missing CONFLICTs

1. **“Mark marginal (all should be)” contradicts the `/tʃ dʒ/` row**, which concludes “so not marginal in the South.” The heading or the row must change.
2. **§2.2 says `/st sp sk sl sr/` are licit onsets, while §3.1/§8.6 assigns prothetic `y-` to s+C.** The digest explains that the vowel is often unpronounced and perhaps structural, but it never turns this into one deterministic surface rule. A rule file cannot simultaneously treat the same clusters as unconditionally licit and repair them without an explicit stress/lexical/orthographic condition.
3. **The formal §3.1 rule covers only `#s{p,t,k}`, while §8.6 says the source rule is s+any consonant** and applies it to `/sm sn sl sr/`. The formal rule and mismatch table disagree in scope.
4. **The coda list assigns *gwallt* to both `/lt/` and `/ɬt/`.** Welsh ⟨ll⟩ is `/ɬ/`; this is an inventory/transcription contradiction, not a dialect conflict.
5. **Missing CONFLICT in §8.1:** the historical “foreign palatal → `/ʃ/`” layer conflicts directly with the modern Southern `/tʃ dʒ/` layer. §1 and §3 record the period split, but §8.1’s universal wording suppresses it where the Irish mapping decision is actually made.
6. **§4.1 says length must be assigned after stress and “not carried from the input,” while §3.7/§4.3/§8.7 say loan length may be preserved and may attract stress.** The digest records the underlying source conflict but never specifies ordering: preserve source length and then choose stress, or assign stress and then Welsh length. This is a rule-ordering contradiction for implementation purposes.
7. **§8.9 turns “Welsh loans enter the Welsh mutation system as radicals” into “reverse Irish lenition before adaptation.”** That conclusion conflicts with §8’s own opening statement that there is no Irish→Welsh evidence and that conclusions are analogical. It must be demoted to an explicit source-side design option.
8. **Powyseg versus Llanwrtyd is not yet a valid dialect conflict.** There is a real conflict between the project’s Southern inventory choice and Breit’s classification of traditional Y Bowyseg with North, but no source connects Llanwrtyd to Y Bowyseg. The digest currently conflates an administrative location with a dialect assignment.

## 5. Fitness for purpose

1. **The §2 cluster inventory is not ready to encode.** Explicit class statements, lexical existence, typo repair, Northern evidence, and pure inference are mixed. A rule writer needs separate fields such as `explicitly_licensed`, `lexically_attested_only`, `northern_only`, and `unverified`.
2. **The modern/historical switch must be resolved before rule writing.** The digest warns well at §3’s opening, but Parry still dominates the data and several later bullets/§8 mappings drop the period label. A default modern generator must not emit medieval `/tʃ,dʒ → ʃ/`, final stop voicing, mutation-backformation, or initial `l/r` fortition as categorical rules.
3. **Do not use “Llanwrtyd is in Powys” as evidence for Northern/Powyseg rules.** The held sources establish neither Llanwrtyd’s traditional dialect nor its patterning. The PHOIBLE segment row looks Southern, but the local phonotactics remain unverified.
4. **The broad/slender mapping is still entirely a design choice.** The digest offers four options but no deterministic selection. That is acceptable for a digest, but a rule writer is stuck until the project chooses one and records that it is an overlay, not an attested Welsh rule.
5. **Irish `/ɣ, ç, iə, uə, mn-/` remain unresolved.** The sources provide historical analogies or inventory-nearest options, not loan rules. These must stay explicit design decisions.
6. **Stress and length ordering is unresolved and load-bearing.** The system cannot implement both “Welsh assigns length after stress” and “source long vowels attract/retain stress” without a precedence rule and an exception policy.
7. **The final C+sonorant repair is the strongest usable Southern rule**, but its alternatives need deterministic conditioning beyond the well-supported antepenult case. The sources say “tendency,” “may,” and “occasionally”; the digest currently formats them more categorically than the evidence warrants.
8. **`attested.csv` is excellent in size but weak for phonetic testing.** 653/751 rows are historical orthographic correspondences with blank IPA. It can test spelling/process provenance, not a modern segmental transducer, unless the modern subset is isolated.
9. **Correct the four Wood page references before automated traceability work.** They currently point to p.233; the actual modern loan table is p.235.
10. **The sources could support a cleaner evidence table without new acquisition.** In particular, `williams1994` supplies an exact Southern epenthesis environment; Breit supplies explicit onset-class constraints; Morris Jones supplies the broad s+C prothesis statement; these should be separated from the assembled lexical lists rather than merged into them.

## 6. Verdict

### Required fixes before use

1. Rebuild §2’s onset/coda claims by evidence level: move typo-corrected `/fr/` and single-token `/dl/` out of **STATED**, remove or source `/vl/ gwefl`, correct *gwallt* to `/ɬt/`, and mark the Southern `CC` maximum/sonority generalisations as unattested inferences.
2. Make every Parry-derived executable claim explicitly historical; rewrite §8.1 so ME/early-NE `/tʃ dʒ → ʃ/` is not presented as the modern or universal Welsh treatment.
3. Remove the claimed Welsh rule “un-lenite Irish before adaptation.” Treat source-side mutation/genitive normalization as an Irish-side design decision.
4. Weaken §8.7’s long-vowel rule to the source’s actual strength: modern loans provide a usual lenition pattern with very limited examples, not a categorical Irish mapping.
5. Separate traditional **Y Bowyseg** from modern administrative **Powys**. State that Breit groups Y Bowyseg with North/Mid Welsh, but Llanwrtyd’s dialect affiliation and phonotactics are unverified.
6. Resolve the s+C scope/ordering contradiction: the formal rule covers only s+stop, while §8 applies it to all s+C, and the same clusters are also listed as licit. Encode pronunciation versus spelling and the stress condition explicitly.
7. Correct the four `attested.csv` citations for *map, siop, mat, cloc* from `wood1988 p.233` to `wood1988 p.235`.
8. Choose and document rule ordering for source vowel length versus Welsh stress/length assignment before generating names.

### Suggestions

- Add per-member citations and IPA to every assembled cluster list.
- Split `attested.csv` into modern-rule and historical-evidence views without discarding either layer.
- Preserve optionality words from the sources (`tendency`, `may`, `occasionally`, `usually`) until the project deliberately chooses deterministic defaults.
- Keep the current explicit “no Irish→Welsh evidence” warning; it is accurate and important.
