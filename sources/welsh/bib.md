# Welsh (Southern) — source bibliography

Assembled 2026-08-24 per `../ACQUISITION-BRIEF.md`. Open access only. Section numbers (§1–§8)
refer to `../DIGEST-TEMPLATE.md`.

Dialect note: the target is **Southern Welsh** (PHOIBLE InvID 2406, Llanwrtyd). Most open
phonetic work on Welsh is Northern (Bangor/Anglesey/Llŷn). Sources below are tagged
*(South)*, *(North)*, or *(both/general)* so the digest can weight them. The two genuinely
Southern-focused items are `liu2018` and `williams1995` (and `parry1923`, whose loan data is
tagged N./S. Wales throughout).

## Used (open access)

### wiki-cy-phon — Wikipedia, "Welsh phonology"
- Full citation: *Welsh phonology*, English Wikipedia, revision fetched 2026-08-24.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_phonology
- Access: OA (CC BY-SA). Verification: confirmed.
- Files: `wiki-Welsh_phonology.wikitext`, `wiki-Welsh_phonology.html`, `wiki-Welsh_phonology.txt`
- Covers: §1 inventory incl. loan-only /z ʒ dʒ tʃ/ and voiceless sonorants; §2 gemination after
  stressed vowels, the *pobl → [ˈpɔbɔl]* / *ffenestr → [ˈfɛnɛst]* / *ewythr → [ˈewɨrθ]* repair
  triad (epenthesis / liquid deletion / metathesis) — the single most digest-ready §3 passage
  found; §4 penultimate stress, exceptions, and the full vowel-length-by-following-consonant
  rule with explicit North/South splits; §6 mutations, i-affection, a-affection. Unusually
  well-sourced article; its bibliography is where several items below came from.

### wiki-cy-orth — Wikipedia, "Welsh orthography"
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_orthography — OA, confirmed.
- Files: `wiki-Welsh_orthography.{wikitext,html,txt}`
- Covers: §5 grapheme→phoneme conventions, digraphs, the *w/y* vowel-or-consonant problem,

### wiki-cy-lang — Wikipedia, "Welsh language" (§Phonology and neighbours)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language — OA, confirmed.
- Files: `wiki-Welsh_language.{wikitext,html,txt}`
- Covers: §0 dialect geography (the N/S divide the digest must pick a side of); §1 summary
  inventory; §6 sketch morphology. Lowest-value of the three; keep for dialect framing.

### wood1988 — Wood, "Vowel Quantity and Syllable Structure in Welsh"
- Full citation: Wood, Sidney A. J. (1988). "Vowel Quantity and Syllable Structure in Welsh."
  *Working Papers* 33, 229–236. Lund University, Dept. of Linguistics.
- URL: https://journals.lub.lu.se/index.php/LWPL/article/download/2625/2200
  (landing page https://journals.lub.lu.se/LWPL/article/view/2625)
- Access: OA. Verification: **confirmed with a correction** — the baseline bibliography dates
  this 1986 and gives the swphonetics.com URL; that URL is now **404** and the paper is
  1988 (Working Papers 33), not 1986. The Lund journal copy is complete (pp. 229–236).
- Files: `wood1988.pdf`, `wood1988.txt`
- Covers: §2/§4 the core claim that Welsh vowel quantity is *predictable* from syllable
  structure (long V in open stressed syllables, short V + long C otherwise), with a
  consonant-length table; §3 explicit remarks on how loanwords are accommodated, including
  final /p t k/ occurring "today ... only in modern loans" and retention of source long
  vowels. Cites Fowkes 1949 on initial mutation of loanwords (not open, see below).

### parry1923 — Parry-Williams, *The English Element in Welsh*
- Full citation: Parry-Williams, T. H. (1923). *The English Element in Welsh: A Study of
  English Loan-Words in Welsh.* Cymmrodorion Record Series No. X. London: Honourable Society
  of Cymmrodorion. 306 pp.
- URL: https://archive.org/details/englishelementin00parruoft
  (PDF: .../englishelementin00parruoft.pdf · text: .../englishelementin00parruoft_djvu.txt)
- Access: public-domain. Verification: confirmed (full scan + OCR, no borrow restriction).
- Files: `parry1923.pdf` (21 MB), `parry1923.txt` (OCR)
- Covers: **the gold set for §7 and the evidence base for §3.** Part I "Sounds and Spellings"
  is organised exactly as an adaptation grammar: ch. II OE loans; ch. III–IV every English
  vowel and diphthong with its Welsh outcomes (§§7–75); ch. V English consonants (§§76–131)
  including *§84* initial s+stop takes a prothetic *y-* (*scarlet > ysgarlat*, *steward >
  ystiwart*, *standard > ystondardd* — though Parry-Williams notes the prothetic vowel is
  "hardly ever pronounced" colloquially unless stressed), *§80 kn- > cn-*, *§81
  prosthetic vowel*, *§78 initial provection*, *§83 v- > b-/m-*, *§89 w- > gw-*, *§90 wh- >
  chw-*, medial and final consonant changes, *§130–131* addition/loss of final consonants.
  Hundreds of attested English→Welsh pairs in Welsh orthography (no IPA on either side;
  dialect tagged "N. Wales"/"S. Wales", roughly per the author's own caveat).
  Caveat: 1923 OCR of a philological text — mining it will need care with italics and
  the author's own notation (`cs` = italic *ae*).

### buczek2014 — Buczek-Zawiła, accent accommodation in Welsh borrowings from English
- Full citation: Buczek-Zawiła, Anita (2014). "Everything Must Have Its Place: Accent
  Accommodation in Modern Welsh Borrowings from English." *Studia Linguistica Universitatis
  Iagellonicae Cracoviensis* 131, 335–351. doi:10.4467/20834624SL.14.020.2727
- URL: https://ejournals.eu/pliki_artykulu_czasopisma/pelny_tekst/19e0f779-2d17-41c1-bc9b-32081430f886/pobierz
- Access: OA. Verification: confirmed (TLS chain needs `curl -k`; content legitimate).
- Files: `buczek2014-accent-accommodation.pdf`, `.txt`
- Covers: §3/§4 — what happens to English source stress when a word enters Welsh's fixed
  penultimate system, with modern loan data. The most on-point *modern* loanword-adaptation
  paper found open; complements parry1923 (historical) on the prosodic side.

### breit2019 — Breit, *Welsh Mutation and Strict Modularity*
- Full citation: Breit, Florian (2019). *Welsh Mutation and Strict Modularity.* PhD thesis,
  University College London.
- URL: https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10087726/1/Thesis%20(colour).pdf
- Access: OA (UCL Discovery). Verification: confirmed, full text.
- Files: `breit2019-thesis.pdf`, `breit2019-thesis.txt`
- Covers: §2 branching-onset well-formedness with explicit illicit clusters (e.g. *[dʃ]*,
  fricative–fricative onsets, the /tl/ problem); §4 penultimate stress and stress shift under
  suffixation, vowel length tied to stress; §6 the mutation system in depth (the best open
  treatment). Theory-heavy (Element Theory) — mine the data and the statements of
  well-formedness, skip the representations.

### breit-harris2014 — Breit & Harris, review of Hannahs (2013)
- Full citation: Breit, F. & Harris, J. (2014). Review of S. J. Hannahs, *The Phonology of
  Welsh* (OUP 2013). *Journal of Linguistics* (author manuscript).
- URL: https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10087870/1/breit-harris2014-final-submitted.pdf
  (mirror: https://florian.me.uk/resources/files/files/publications/breit-harris2014.pdf)
- Access: OA. Verification: confirmed.
- Files: `breit-harris2014.pdf`, `.txt`
- Covers: **partial substitute for the paywalled Hannahs 2013.** Summarises Hannahs's
  syllabification, complex-onset/coda analysis (codas complex only word-finally; at most one
  medial coda C), the bisyllabic/bimoraic minimal word, and penultimate stress — with the
  reviewers' disagreements flagged. Use it to know *what Hannahs says* where we cannot cite
  him directly; treat as second-hand.

### williams1995 — B. Williams, TTS for Welsh and Welsh English *(South)*
- Full citation: Williams, Briony (1995). "Text-to-Speech Synthesis for Welsh and Welsh
  English." CSTR, University of Edinburgh (Eurospeech).
- URL: https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/downloads/publications/1995/Williams_1995_a.pdf
  (also Edinburgh Research Archive: https://era.ed.ac.uk/items/a72544e0-5509-4164-a022-fd1a5df2510b)
- Access: OA. Verification: confirmed.
- Files: `williams1995-tts.pdf`, `.txt`
- Covers: §1/§3/§5 — an explicit **South Welsh** phoneme set (32 C + 19 V = 51 segments),
  tagged for which segments are loan-only, which are restricted-context, and which extra
  English segments had to be added because Welsh has no equivalent; i.e. a ready-made
  substitution table for English→Welsh. **Caveat: the PDF's phoneme tables use a symbol font
  that does not survive `pdftotext` (Tables 1–2 come out as garbage) — they must be read
  from the PDF pages visually.**

### williams1994 — B. Williams, Welsh letter-to-sound rules
- Full citation: Williams, Briony (1994). "Welsh letter-to-sound rules: rewrite rules and
  two-level rules compared." *Computer Speech and Language* 8(3) (author copy).
- URL: https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/downloads/publications/1994/Williams_1994_a.pdf
- Access: OA (author copy; the journal version is paywalled at Elsevier). Confirmed.
- Files: `williams1994-lts.pdf`, `.txt`
- Covers: §5 a 356-rule orthography→phoneme system, applied in three passes — (1) insert
  epenthetic vowels, (2) assign stress and vowel length, (3) convert graphemes. That
  three-pass order is itself a compact statement of §2/§4/§5 and is directly reusable as
  the shape of the romanization/pronunciation layer.

### williams1983 — B. Williams, *Stress in Modern Welsh* (PhD)
- Full citation: Williams, Briony J. (1983). *Stress in Modern Welsh.* PhD thesis, University
  of Cambridge. doi:10.17863/CAM.16507, hdl:1810/250821
- URL (landing): https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/250821
  PDF (90 MB, not downloaded — over the size we want in-repo):
  https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/479cd032-8c9b-4a90-a57f-c14baa65d564/content
- Access: OA. Verification: confirmed.
- Files: `williams1983-stress-thesis.txt` (the repository's own OCR text bundle, 442 KB)
- Covers: §4 the standard reference on Welsh stress: the acoustically odd Welsh pattern
  (stressed penult is *shorter*, lower-amplitude, with high pitch on the *following*
  unstressed final), consonant lengthening after stress, and the historical final→penult
  shift. Cited throughout the Wikipedia article. OCR quality is mediocre; check any figure
  against the PDF URL above.

### iosad2017 — Iosad, length and quality in Welsh vowels
- Full citation: Iosad, Pavel (2017). "The phonologisation of redundancy: length and quality
  in Welsh vowels." *Phonology* 34(1), 121–162 (author copy).
- URL: https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/28667740/paper.pdf
- Access: OA author copy (journal version paywalled at CUP). Confirmed.
- Files: `iosad2017-length-quality.pdf`, `.txt`
- Covers: §1/§4 — the North/South split in how vowel length and vowel quality trade off,

  Directly relevant to deciding what a Southern-Welsh length rule should output.

### morrisjones1913 — J. Morris Jones, *A Welsh Grammar, Historical and Comparative*
- Full citation: Morris Jones, John (1913). *A Welsh Grammar, Historical and Comparative:
  Phonology and Accidence.* Oxford: Clarendon Press.
- URL: https://archive.org/details/welshgrammarhist00joneuoft
- Access: public-domain. Confirmed (OCR text downloaded; the PDF is large and was not taken).
- Files: `morrisjones1913-grammar.txt`
- Covers: §2 initial combinations and consonant groups; §4 accent and vowel quantity;

  Caveat: 1913 notation and terminology; conclusions on prehistory are superseded.

## Not used (paywalled / login-walled / blocked)

- **Hannahs, S. J. (2013). *The Phonology of Welsh.* OUP.** — [Buy]. The modern standard:
  syllabification, the full onset/coda cluster statement, the bisyllabic/bimoraic minimal
  word, penultimate stress. **This is the largest single loss.** Partly compensated by
  `breit-harris2014` (a detailed review that restates his analyses second-hand) and by the
  Wikipedia article, which cites Hannahs page-by-page for gemination, vowel length and the
  *pobl/ffenestr/ewythr* repairs — so several of his claims are recoverable *with* page
  numbers, but at one remove.
- **Awbery, G. M. (1984). "Phonotactic constraints in Welsh."** In Ball & Jones (eds.),
  *Welsh Phonology: Selected Readings*, Cardiff: UWP, pp. 65–104. — Verified to exist as
  described. The book is on archive.org (`archive.org/details/welshphonologyse0000unse`) but
  is **controlled digital lending: borrow requires an archive.org account**, so excluded.
  What we lose: the one dedicated statement of Welsh phonotactic constraints, i.e. the
  explicit licit-onset/licit-coda lists §2 wants.
- **Awbery, G. (2009). "Welsh."** In Ball & Müller (eds.), *The Celtic Languages* 2nd ed.,
  Routledge, 373–440. — book on archive.org under lending restriction; a standard descriptive
  chapter incl. Southern dialect detail.
- **Ball, M. J. & Williams, B. (2001). *Welsh Phonetics.* Lampeter: Mellen.** — [Buy], no
  preview found. Would give a phonetic description of both dialects.
- **Czerniak, P. T. (2015). "Word-Initial Clusters in Welsh: A Typological Analysis."** In
  Bondaruk et al. (eds.), *Young Minds vs. Old Questions in Linguistics*, Lublin: KUL /
  Institute of East-Central Europe. — **Author and venue identified** (the baseline
  bibliography left it anonymous). Available only on ResearchGate and academia.edu, both
  login-walled; no repository or author-page mirror found (KUL has no open deposit of it).
  Same for Czerniak's companion papers ("Welsh vowels — element distribution and
  interaction", "The Fate of Word-Final Clusters in North Welsh", "Diphthongs in the North of
  Wales"). What we lose: an explicit typology of licit word-initial clusters (TR vs ST/STR)
  and the syncope-derived clusters that violate sonority sequencing — the second-biggest gap
  after Hannahs. (Note it is Bangor-dialect, i.e. Northern, data.)
- **Fowkes, R. A. (1949). "Initial mutation of loanwords in Welsh." *Word* 5, 203–212.** —
  paywalled; cited by wood1988 as the source on how loans are fitted into the mutation
  system.
- **Fomin & Matasović, "Loanwords in Welsh: frequency analysis of Cronfa Electroneg o
  Gymraeg"** (*Studia Celto-Slavica* III, 2010) — every mirror is either academia.edu
  (login) or Cloudflare-403. Would have given corpus frequencies of loan strata.
- **Jackson, K. H. (1953). *Language and History in Early Britain.*** — archive.org copy is
  lending-restricted. The standard treatment of Irish–British contact.
- **Melville Richards, "Irish Settlements in South-West Wales"** and the Celtic-studies
  journal literature generally (*Bulletin of the Board of Celtic Studies*, *Studia Celtica*,
  *Y Cymmrodor*, *Trans. Hon. Soc. Cymmrodorion*) — these are digitised and open at
  **Welsh Journals Online (journals.library.wales)**, but that whole site is currently behind
  a Cloudflare bot challenge that blocks curl, WebFetch (403) and a headless browser alike.
  **This is a blocked resource, not a missing one:** a human with an ordinary browser can
  almost certainly download these PDFs. If the Irish→Welsh gap matters, this is the place to
  look, by hand.
- **BydTermCymru** (Welsh Government terminology/style guidance incl. foreign country names),
  `gov.wales/bydtermcymru` — HTTP 403 to automated fetches (WAF, not a login wall).
- **Journal of Celtic Linguistics** (e.g. Hammond 2019 on voiceless nasals) — paywalled.
- Considered and rejected as low-value: Pedersen's *Vergleichende Grammatik* (1909, open on
  archive.org, 53 MB) and the *Archaeologia Cambrensis* "Welsh or Irish Celtic" glossary
  series (1882–83, open) — both give Welsh/Irish **cognate** sets, which are not adaptation
  data and would mislead §7 if mined as such.

## Notes for the digest author

1. **Dialect discipline.** The best-instrumented open sources are Northern (JIPA, Czerniak,
   Fynes-Clinton, most acoustics). For Southern, lean on `liu2018`, `williams1995`,
   `iosad2017` (explicit N/S contrasts), `mayr-davies2011` (both dialects measured), and the
   Wikipedia article's N/S notes. Southern Welsh notably lacks /ɨ/ (matching PHOIBLE 2406),
   has long vowels in some environments where the North has short, and in the south-east
   loses /h/ and merges /r̥/ into /r/.
2. **§3 is well served for English→Welsh and badly served for anything else.** `parry1923`
   plus the Wikipedia repair passage plus `buczek2014` give epenthesis, prothesis
   (*ysgol*-type), deletion, metathesis, provection and stress re-assignment with examples.
   No open source gives a comparable account of adaptation from a non-English donor.
3. **§7 count is achievable**: `parry1923` alone can supply well over 30 rows; the constraint
   is that both sides are in orthography, not IPA, so `target_ipa`/`source_ipa` will often be
   blank per `../ATTESTED-FORMAT.md`.
