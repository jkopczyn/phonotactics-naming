# Adversarial review — Dutch digest

## 1. Citation spot-check

I checked 33 cited claims, weighted toward the cluster inventory, repair rules, and Irish-facing mappings. Totals: **22 VERIFIED / 4 MISQUOTED / 2 NOT FOUND / 5 OVERSTATED**.

1. **VERIFIED — Belgian /x ɣ/.** Digest: “Belgian Dutch has a voiced and voiceless velar fricative” (lines 50–53). Source: “Belgian Dutch has a voiced and voiceless velar fricative: these are absent in the Netherlands” [verhoeven2005 PDF p.244].
2. **VERIFIED — fricative devoicing rates.** Digest: “70% of word-initial and 56% of intervocalic fricatives … no vocal-fold vibration; word-final fricatives are always voiceless” (lines 56–59). Source gives exactly those percentages and “Word-final fricatives are always voiceless” [verhoeven2005 PDF p.244].
3. **VERIFIED — Belgian rhotics.** Digest: alveolar/uvular free variation and devoicing word-finally/before a voiceless stop (lines 60–65). Source: “free variation between an alveolar and uvular trill” and “voiced in all positions, except word-finally … and when followed by a voiceless stop” [verhoeven2005 PDF p.245].
4. **VERIFIED — twelve monophthongs and three diphthongs.** Digest lines 87–89. Source says exactly “twelve monophthongs and three diphthongs” and supplies the chart [verhoeven2005 PDF p.245].
5. **VERIFIED — schwa restrictions.** Digest: schwa cannot bear stress, a lexical word needs a full vowel, and schwa does not begin a polysyllabic lexical word (lines 96–101). Source: “lexical words must have stress and schwa cannot carry stress”; “lexical words that only contain schwa do not exist”; “schwas do not occur word-initially in polysyllabic words” [taalportaal-nuclei lines 9ff.].
6. **VERIFIED — onset maximum.** Digest: at most CCC, with /s/ first (lines 127–130). Source: “Dutch syllable onsets consist of maximally three segments. The first segment … has to be an /s/” [taalportaal-onset-clusters-3c §quickinfo].
7. **VERIFIED — singleton /ŋ/ restriction.** Digest: every singleton except /ŋ/; medial /ŋ/ follows a B-class vowel and precedes schwa (lines 137–141). Source: “Any singleton … with the exception of … /ŋ/” and medial examples are “following B-class vowels” with “the second vowel always being schwa” [taalportaal-onsets-simple lines 8, 139].
8. **VERIFIED — onset/nucleus exclusions.** Digest: `*/hə ji ʋy ʋø/` (lines 144–146). Source lists exactly `/*hə/`, `/*ji/`, `/*ʋy/`, and `/*ʋø/` [taalportaal-onsets-simple line 144].
9. **VERIFIED — sonorant+obstruent onsets.** Digest: “categorically banned” (lines 169–170). Source: this combination “is strictly ruled out in Dutch” [taalportaal-onset-clusters-2c line 257].
10. **VERIFIED — two-coronal/two-labial exclusions.** Digest lists `*tl *dl *zl` and `*pʋ *bʋ *fʋ *vʋ` (lines 171–173). Source lists those exact sequences as prohibited [taalportaal-onset-clusters-2c line 269].
11. **VERIFIED — CCC inventory.** Digest lists native `/spl spr str sxr/` and loan `/skl skr skʋ/` (lines 181–183). Taalportaal’s complete table contains exactly those seven sequences and marks the latter three loanwords [taalportaal-onset-clusters-3c Table 1].
12. **VERIFIED — initial glottal attack.** Digest: obligatory before a stressed word-initial vowel, optional before an unstressed one (lines 187–190). Source states exactly this [taalportaal-onsetless-syllables line 93]. Verhoeven independently limits [ʔ] to “word-initial position as a strong attack of vowels” [PDF p.244].
13. **VERIFIED — nucleus/coda contract.** Digest lines 194–199. Source: A-class vowels/diphthongs may be open or take one coda and “cannot precede a branching coda”; B-class vowels require one and allow at most two [taalportaal-nuclei line 176].
14. **VERIFIED — final consonant maximum.** Digest: medial maximum two, word-final maximum five (lines 202–205). Source gives “two” word-medially and “up to five consonants” word-finally [taalportaal-codas line 10].
15. **VERIFIED — nasal+obstruent codas.** Digest: no nasal+fricative in one coda; nasal+stop must be homorganic (lines 218–221). Source says exactly that [taalportaal-codas line 264].
16. **VERIFIED — tense/lax fricative co-occurrence.** Digest: A-class with `/v z ɣ/`, B-class with `/f s x/`, followed by a substantial exception list (lines 234–250). Taalportaal states those pairings and documents the listed loans, dialect-merger cases, lexical exceptions, and diphthong exceptions [taalportaal-coda-cooccurrence-restrictions, relevant subsections].
17. **VERIFIED — `*VːCC[-coronal]`.** Digest lines 252–266. Kager & Pater state that long-vowel clusters are rare where “the second member of the cluster is a non-coronal” and give *paal/palm/*paalm and the other cited paradigms [kager-pater2012 p.6, ex.2].
18. **VERIFIED — /h/ and /ŋ/ coda distribution.** Digest lines 270–275. Source: “/h/ cannot occur in codas”; `/ŋ/` “can exclusively occur in codas” and only after B-class vowels [taalportaal-codas line 9].
19. **MISQUOTED — loans keeping initial /pn/.** Digest: “Loans may simply keep an illicit onset … *pneumatisch* /pn-/” and cites Booij 2011 (lines 343–345). Booij says only that Dutch words “begin with pn- in the spelling” and that the spelling marks foreign origin; the later statement that speakers have “no difficulty in pronouncing … pneumatisk” is explicitly about **Norwegian**, not Dutch [booij2011-msc pp.2052, 2056]. Taalportaal elsewhere transcribes *pneumonie* with /pn-/, but the citation actually attached to this repair claim does not.
20. **MISQUOTED — schwa-epenthesis environment.** Digest rule: `∅ → ə / [+liquid] __ C[-coronal]` (lines 353–354), yet its own examples include /rn/ (*hoorn, urn*), where C2 is coronal. Source says insertion occurs in a **non-homorganic** complex coda after /l/ or /r/, and separately blocks a coronal **obstruent** `/s/` or `/t/` [taalportaal-schwa-epenthesis-deletion lines 95, 104]. `C[-coronal]` is not the source rule.
21. **OVERSTATED — t-deletion as a general three-consonant/loan repair.** Digest places the rule under “Three-consonant sequences” (lines 375–381). Source evidence is narrower: obligatory deletion in specific native morphological environments before diminutive *-je* and *-s/-st*, optional deletion in compounds/phrases, and blocking after a sonorant [taalportaal-casual-speech exx.9–11]. It does not establish deletion as the repair for an arbitrary illicit donor CCC sequence.
22. **VERIFIED — Belgian foreign /g/ options, with a needed scope warning.** Digest’s examples and survey numbers (lines 386–388) match the sources: Flemish *baguette* has native-g percentages 63–88 and zero [k], young Flemish *spaghetti* is 100% native-g, and *drugs* has 14/15 spelling pronunciations [loan-kleinbreukink1999-culinair p.276; loan-gerritsen1995-engels p.16]. The digest should still say “not observed in these Belgian samples,” rather than convert zero observations into an exceptionless dialect rule.
23. **OVERSTATED — “Belgian keeps” French nasal vowels.** Digest table says “Belgian keeps them (integration)” (line 398), but the same row concedes that both North and Belgium mostly denasalize *plafond, campagne, restaurant*. Theissen’s evidence is word-specific: Belgian speakers have a much larger nasal-preserving set, not a categorical preserve-vs-denasalize grammar [loan-theissen2006-nasalen §§1–3].
24. **OVERSTATED — final devoicing “without exception.”** Digest: “an active rule, applied to loans without exception” (lines 406–407). Taalportaal’s wording is weaker: voiced obstruents are “generally not found in syllable-final position,” and its incomplete-neutralization section explicitly notes disputed small phonetic differences [taalportaal-final-devoicing lines 8, 364]. The cited page does support syllable-final devoicing and the examples, but not the absolute loan-specific wording.
25. **OVERSTATED — vowel length is categorically reinterpreted and ‘forced by the coda.’** Digest lines 440–441. The cited *calme/palme* pairs establish old loans with Dutch /ɑ/ before /lm/; they do not establish a general adaptation algorithm for Irish length. The digest’s own data show competing preservation/shortening and token variation (*team, pool*; *slow motion*; *no way*), so this must be a proposed deterministic policy, not a sourced rule.
26. **VERIFIED — stress generalizations as tendencies.** Digest lines 466–493 accurately reproduces Taalportaal’s three-syllable window, schwa, closed-penult, superheavy-final, final-diphthong, and weak final-BC tendencies. The source explicitly warns: “none of these generalizations are exceptionless” [taalportaal-stress-generalizations §quickinfo].
27. **OVERSTATED — deterministic ordered stress procedure.** Digest turns those tendencies into “The practical rule (ordered; apply to a novel word)” (lines 500–513). The source classifies them as strong/solid/weak tendencies with counterexamples; it does not supply this precedence ordering. In addition, the procedure does not state when source stress (the loan default below) overrides it.
28. **VERIFIED — loanwords generally preserve source stress.** Digest line 516 quotes the source accurately. Source: “the large majority of loanwords follow the stress pattern of the source language,” while warning that observations about ongoing shifts are impressionistic [taalportaal-stress-loanwords line 8].
29. **NOT FOUND — *-achtig* as “one of only two consonant-initial suffixes.”** Digest line 620. Neither cited source supports that description. The local final-devoicing source says the opposite: *-aardig* and *-achtig* are the two exceptional **vowel-initial** suffixes that trigger devoicing, in addition to consonant-initial suffixes [taalportaal-final-devoicing, “The influence of suffixes”].
30. **MISQUOTED — “Gemination: none/never.”** Digest says “Gemination: none” (line 291) and “Gemination: never” (line 455), but the cited source says only that geminates are barred **within prosodic words**; across compounds/phrases degemination is optional and “geminates may occur,” with [bɛrxːɛit], [vɛrːɑsə(n)], and [nɛpːlɑnt] [taalportaal-degemination §quickinfo, exx.2–3].
31. **MISQUOTED — hiatus repair stated without its conditioning.** Digest: “after a front vowel [j], after a back vowel [w]” (lines 297–299). Source says [j] after **unrounded** front vowels, [ʋ] after back vowels, [ɥ] after front rounded vowels, and no transitional glide after /a/ or schwa; those latter cases use stress-conditioned [ʔ] or retain hiatus [taalportaal-hiatus-resolution exx.2–3].
32. **VERIFIED — Cj has target-internal/name-shaped support.** Digest lines 665–672. The allophony page gives the cited palatal outputs, Verhoeven gives [c ɲ ʎ] in the stated environments, and Booij explicitly says initial `/pj tj kj/` occurs in borrowed male names *Pjotr, Tjeerd, Kjeld* without pronunciation problems [booij2011-msc p.2056]. This supports pronounceability, not an adaptation mapping from Irish palatalization.
33. **NOT FOUND — direct Irish `/əi əu/ → /ɛi ɔu/`.** Digest: “map directly” (lines 720–722), citing only Verhoeven’s Dutch diphthong inventory. Verhoeven shows that `/ɛi œy ɔu/` exist; it says nothing about Irish or about adaptation of schwa-initial diphthongs. This is a plausible fallback/design decision, not an attested mapping.

## 2. Uncited or under-cited claims

- The digest repeatedly uses non-resolvable shorthand instead of a bibliography key and locator: `[p.244]`, `[ibid.]`, `[wold]`, `[wold, van der Sijs]`, `[taalportaal-codas]`, `[names-wiki-translit]`, and `[vandersijs1996 p.57]`. The template requires `[key p.N]` or `[key §Section]`. This is especially serious for WOLD-derived process claims because WOLD supplies no donor IPA.
- “The fricative voicing contrast … is **phonologically stable** in Belgian” (line 55) is an interpretation of a phonetic paper whose wording is “held traditionally” and which immediately reports extensive devoicing. Cite a phonological source or soften it.
- “Dutch orthography is … shallow” (line 533) is uncited and not operationally useful. More importantly, the project asks for a romanization an English reader can pronounce approximately; native Dutch spelling does not meet that requirement without a second reader-oriented convention.
- “No final-vowel addition, no prothesis is reported anywhere” (line 435) is an uncited corpus-wide negative and lacks `(unattested)`/`not covered` wording tied to the searched sources.
- “Unstressed reduced vowels in the donor are **typically** restored from spelling” (line 449) rests on two television tokens (*tattoo, community*). This is under-cited and overstated as a general rule.
- WOLD rows are repeatedly used to infer donor-side processes despite blank donor IPA: *ministre* “final /tʁ/ cluster broken,” *templum* “schwa epenthesis,” and *maíz* “hiatus resolved with glide.” WOLD verifies the orthographic pair and Dutch IPA only; the process analysis needs a donor-pronunciation/historical source or must be labelled inferred.
- §8 says Irish coda /h/ “must be deleted or moved” (line 694). The citation establishes only that Dutch bars coda /h/; neither repair is attested. Mark both `(unattested)` design options.
- §8’s exact `/iə/ → [i.jə]`, `/uə/ → [u.wə]` proposal (lines 716–719) is a target-internal extrapolation. Taalportaal supports [j] after /i/ and [ʋ], not [w], after /u/ in hiatus, but not Irish loan adaptation. Label it fallback and use the Belgian-normalized segment.
- “Irish short vowel is already B-class” (line 715) is unsupported and false as an implementation shortcut. Irish short /i e o u a ə/ still require explicit quality mappings; Dutch schwa is not a B-class full vowel.
- §6 line 620’s *-achtig* onset classification is not only uncited by the attached references but incorrect (see spot-check 29).

## 3. Rule/example consistency

### §3 rule instantiation

- **3.0 adaptation registers:** instantiated in text by *grill, goal, cake, coat, drugs*.
- **3.1 illicit onset:** the “keep it as marked foreign” option has in-text examples, but the /pn/ example is miscited. The **deletion tiebreaker has no adult-loan row and no concrete Dutch loan example**. The `/gr/ → /xr/` and `/g/ → /ɣ/` examples instantiate segment substitution, not repair of a cluster that Dutch bans. A rule-writer still has no attested adult onset-cluster repair.
- **3.2 coda epenthesis:** instantiated by native in-text examples, *single*, and WOLD historical pairs. The exact formal environment is wrong, however, and WOLD does not independently verify donor pronunciations.
- **3.3 t-deletion:** instantiated only by native morphological/casual-speech examples; no `attested.tsv` loan row instantiates it.
- **3.4 substitution table:** every row has an in-text example and most have a TSV row. `/æ/ → /ɛ/` is not deterministic: TSV also has retained /æ/ in *jam, superman,* and *tax*. French nasal preservation is word-specific. These alternatives need conditioning or explicit conflict/policy selection.
- **3.5 edge/process rules:** final devoicing has TSV rows (*headset, rose, cruise, admission*). Voice assimilation, nasal assimilation, /n/-deletion, and glottal attack have in-text native/loan examples but no TSV rows. No final-vowel addition/prothesis is a “not covered” finding, not a rule.
- **3.6 vowel adaptation:** examples exist, but they instantiate multiple competing outcomes, not the categorical “length → class, forced by coda” algorithm.
- **3.7 gemination:** contradicted by its own cited examples: it is barred within a prosodic word but can surface across larger boundaries. Metathesis is correctly “not covered.”

## 4. Internal contradictions and missing CONFLICT lines

1. **Maximal coda/template conflict.** §2’s template has four post-nuclear consonants. The same section says Taalportaal allows five word-finally. LAPSyD’s record itself says `Coda=3` but prints four coda slots. This needs a `CONFLICT:` line and an implementation-level resolution, probably “rhyme coda ≤2 plus up to three coronal appendix positions,” rather than one unexplained maximal template.
2. **Underlying vs surface coda lists.** §2 lists voiced coda clusters such as `-lv -lz -lɣ -rv -rz -rɣ -ld -rd -nd`, then says voiced obstruents never surface in codas. Mark the table explicitly as underlying/lexical and give the surface outputs; otherwise a rule-writer may license impossible voiced surface codas.
3. **“Gemination none” vs geminates in the cited examples.** §2 prints [xː], [rː], and optional boundary geminates; §3 says “never.” Replace with the domain-sensitive rule.
4. **Categorical length policy vs variable data.** §3.6 says length is not carried over and class is forced; §3.4/TSV contain retained source vowels/diphthongs and competing long/short pronunciations. Add `CONFLICT:` and choose a deterministic strand policy.
5. **Categorical `/æ/ → /ɛ/` vs retained /æ/.** The table bolds /ɛ/ as the mapping, while *jam, superman,* and *tax* retain /æ/. Add conditioning or an explicit conflict/fallback priority.
6. **French nasal “integration” vs lexical denasalization.** “Belgian keeps them” conflicts with *plafond, campagne, restaurant*. The rule must be lexical/loan-register-dependent or select a deliberate policy.
7. **Stress algorithm vs source-stress default.** §4 first creates a target-native ordered procedure, then says most loans preserve source stress. State precedence. For the actual Irish input, source stress is available in IPA, so this choice materially changes every output.
8. **`-achtig` classification.** §6 calls it consonant-initial; §3.5’s source classifies it with *-aardig* as a vowel-initial exception that nevertheless triggers devoicing.
9. **TSV field contradiction.** §7 correctly says the French/Latin donor column is stress-marked orthography, while `attested.tsv` labels it `source_ipa`.
10. **Irish coda /h/.** §8 mandates “deleted or moved,” but §3 has neither an attested deletion target nor a movement/metathesis rule. This should remain an explicit unresolved design decision.
11. **Belgian/Netherlandic symbol normalization is unfinished.** The exhaustive onset/coda lists use Netherlandic `/x ɣ ʋ r/` conventions, while §1 defines Belgian targets differently. §8 then calls `/gl gr/` licit “as-is” while discussing `/ɣl ɣr/` and `/xl xr/`. Supply one normalized Belgian phonemic list before rules are encoded.

## 5. Fitness for purpose

Concrete blockers for a deterministic Irish→Dutch rule file:

- **No adult loan repair for illicit initial clusters.** The digest cannot tell the implementation whether to delete C1/C2, epenthesize, or retain marked clusters. Child deletion preferences are not enough. This must stay an explicit policy/fallback rather than masquerade as Dutch loan adaptation.
- **The formal schwa rule is wrong.** Encoding `C[-coronal]` would fail cited `/rn/` examples and conflate non-homorganicity with coronality. Rewrite the environment from the source and state optionality/domain.
- **No ordered repair pipeline.** Syllabification, A/B choice, fricative voicing, epenthesis, assimilation, final devoicing, and stress can feed one another. The digest gives no order and no worked Irish derivation. At minimum, derive *Matánach* and one cluster-heavy form step by step under each unresolved policy.
- **Irish segment/vowel table is incomplete.** §8 discusses consonants narratively but gives no deterministic mappings for voiceless sonorants, coda /h/, palatalized codas, or the Irish monophthong inventory. “Irish short vowel = B-class” cannot replace a vowel-quality table.
- **Broad/slender is correctly left open, but that means the digest is not yet usable.** Pick collapse, onset-only Cj, or vowel colouring before rule generation; each needs explicit coda behavior and romanization.
- **Stress cannot be made deterministic from the cited tendency hierarchy without a policy decision.** Since Irish source stress is provided, specify whether it is preserved by default and exactly which Dutch hard constraints override it.
- **The Belgian target is mixed with Netherlandic evidence without an executable normalization layer.** Convert every source inventory/list/example to one Belgian target symbol set and tag genuinely Netherlandic-only data.
- **WOLD is being asked to prove more than it contains.** It is useful for historical form pairs and Dutch IPA, but donor-side cluster/vowel processes need donor pronunciation or etymological evidence. Mark inferred tags so tests do not treat them as direct observations.
- **Romanization does not satisfy the project goal yet.** Native Dutch spelling is not approximately readable by an English reader for ⟨g, ch, sch, ij/ei, ui, eu, oe, u⟩. Choose either (a) authentic Dutch orthography plus a pronunciation key, or (b) a separate English-reader romanization. The current section documents failure modes but still leaves the generator with one unsuitable output convention.
- **The sources could have supplied a more faithful hiatus rule and domain-sensitive gemination rule.** Both are present locally and should be transcribed fully rather than simplified into incorrect absolutes.

## 6. Verdict

### Required fixes before use

1. Replace the schwa-epenthesis formula with the sourced non-homorganic liquid+C rule, including the `/s t/` block, `/rn/` cases, optionality, and domain.
2. Remove or relabel unsupported deterministic repairs: initial-cluster deletion, coda-/h/ deletion or movement, categorical length→A/B conversion, and direct Irish diphthong mappings. Mark them as explicit design fallbacks until chosen.
3. Resolve the maximal-coda conflict and distinguish true coda positions from coronal appendix positions; label all coda cluster lists underlying vs surface.
4. Correct the domain-sensitive gemination rule and the fully conditioned hiatus rule; delete “gemination never” and the front/back-only glide summary.
5. Make substitution variation executable: add priorities/conditioning for `/æ/`, `/ʌ/`, foreign `/g/`, French nasals, and vowel length, or explicitly choose one strand policy under `CONFLICT:` lines.
6. Set stress precedence between preserved Irish source stress and Dutch native tendencies; do not present the current tendency-derived ordering as sourced and exceptionless.
7. Normalize all Netherlandic phonotactic data and IPA to one Belgian target representation before producing rules.
8. Repair `attested.tsv` provenance semantics in the digest’s use of it: do not treat stress-marked orthography as `source_ipa`, and label WOLD process tags as inferred where donor IPA is absent. The *rapskills* row cannot attest `/æ/ → /ɛ/` as currently encoded.
9. Supply a complete Irish-vowel and absent-segment mapping table, including voiceless sonorants, coda /h/, palatalized codas, and fallback labels.
10. Choose a romanization that meets the English-reader requirement; native Dutch spelling alone does not.
11. Correct *-achtig* to a vowel-initial, non-cohering exception, and replace malformed/shorthand citations with resolvable `bib.md` keys plus page/section locators.

### Suggestions

- Add one worked derivation for *Matánach* and one cluster-heavy Irish name to expose rule ordering and unresolved choices.
- Separate “modern productive loan repair,” “native phonological alternation,” and “historical adaptation” in §3/`attested.tsv`; they are currently mixed as if equally predictive.
- Preserve the useful Belgian survey percentages rather than collapsing them into categorical dialect rules.
