Language name : Egyptian Arabic
ISO 639-3     : arz
Alternate     : 
Classification: Afro-Asiatic, Semitic
Location      : Egypt, especially the lower Nile valley.

COUNTS: consonants=27 basic vowels=5 total vowels=8 C/V ratio=3.375 top C=l top V=ɪ

=== SYLLABLE STRUCTURE ===
Syllcat (cf. WALS ch.12): Complex
Syllabic index (O+N+C) = 4   [Onset=0 Nucleus=2 Coda=2]
Canonical Form      : (C)V(C)(C)
Syllabic Restriction: (C)V(C)(C)
Syllabic notes      : (C)V(C)(C). /χamast/ "five", /katabt/ "I wrote", /zanb/ "guilt"

=== STRESS ===
Stresscat (cf. WALS ch.14/16): Minor
Fixed Stress:    Weight-sensitivity: 
Stress notes: Mitchell (1962) indicates stress falls on final long heavy syllable (CVVC, CVCC), otherwise penultimate syllable is stressed, unless it and the preceding syllable are both CV, when the antepenultimate is stressed.

=== TONE ===
Tonecat: None
Tone notes: None

=== CONSONANT NOTES ===
All consonants can appear geminate. In colloquial Egyptian (Cairo) Arabic only a few words retain /q/, including the name of the city, /el qaː'hera/.

=== VOWEL NOTES ===
Long mid vowels /eː, oː/ historically derive from /ai, au/; /aj, aw/ sequences occur where /j, w/ fill consonant slots in templatic morphology. Watson (2000) suggests that front and back variants of the long and short low vowels (transcribed /æː, a/ here) are developing into contrastive pairs (/æ, æː/, /ɑ, ɑː/) due to loss of a pharyngeal(ized) conditioning context. Here the consensus of 5 long and 3 short vowels indicated by e.g. Tomiche (1964), Cowan (1970) is accepted with qualities as suggested by Thelwall & Sa'adeddin (1990).
