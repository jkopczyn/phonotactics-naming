Language name : Georgian
ISO 639-3     : kat
Alternate     : Kartuli, Gruzinskij
Classification: Kartvelian (South Caucasian)
Location      : Republic of Georgia, plus some nearby areas in Turkey, Iran and Azerbaijan.

COUNTS: consonants=28 basic vowels=5 total vowels=5 C/V ratio=5.6 top C=s top V=a

=== SYLLABLE STRUCTURE ===
Syllcat (cf. WALS ch.12): Complex
Syllabic index (O+N+C) = 7   [Onset=3 Nucleus=1 Coda=3]
Canonical Form      : (C)(C)(C)(C)(C)(C)V(C)(C)(C)
Syllabic Restriction: (C)(C)(C)(C)(C)(C)V(C)(C)(C)
Syllabic notes      : (C)(C)(C)(C)(C)(C)V(C)(C)(C)(C) according to Vogt (1958). Examples /pɾtskβna/ 'peeling' /pɾtkβinβa/ "neigh" /pɾχiβ/ "on foot", /βχetkt/ "we split". Butskhrikidze (2002) presents a theoretical model reducing the basic syllable structure to CCVC based on treating 'harmonic clusters' as single segments and liquids as syllabic. Hewitt (1995: 22) asserts that "there are always as many syllables per word as there are vowels" and "Sonants in consonant-complexes have no syllable-forming capacity".

=== STRESS ===
Stresscat (cf. WALS ch.14/16): Minor
Fixed Stress: Antepenult   Weight-sensitivity: None
Stress notes: Jun et al (2006) support the interpretation of stress as falling on the first syllable of a word, with phrase-level prominences responsible for percepts of stress falling on later syllables. Robins & Waterson (1952) and Hewitt (1985) both remark on the weak nature of Georgian stress but suggest it may fall on other than first syllables. Apparently it has no lexical function.

=== TONE ===
Tonecat: None
Tone notes: None

=== CONSONANT NOTES ===
Place for coronals is based on labelling as alveolar or dental in Robins & Waterson (1952); apart from /n/ Shosted & Chikovani (2006) agree. who also describe as 'most generally' similar to (British) English [ɹ] but Shosted & Chikovani (2006) show a flap /ɾ/. Robins & Waterson (1952) describe as 'more velarized than English "dark l"' before back vowels and velarized also before central /a/. Shosted & Chikovani (2006) transcribe this segment in some cases as a velar lateral. Sources vary on whether back fricatives are velar or uvular, but they are apparently more retracted than the velar plosives at least in back vowel contexts. Robins & Waterson (1952) report that unaspirated plosives and affricates are voiced intervocalically and in some clusters also prevocalically. Shosted & Chikovani (2006) transcribe the series as voiced but show a voice onset delay in initial positions, as does Vicenik (2010) whose study shows that perseveration of voicing occurs to some degree in all three stop series in word-medial intervocalic position.

=== VOWEL NOTES ===
Robins & Waterson (1952) describe the mid vowels as 'between half-close and 'half-open'. Formant measurements in Shosted & Chikovani (2006) suggest they are higher mid despite using the symbols [ɛ, ɔ] for them. Vowel sequences form separate syllables.
