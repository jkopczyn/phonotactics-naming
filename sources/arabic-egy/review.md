# Adversarial review: Egyptian Arabic digest

## 1. Citation spot-check

**Sample: 40 claims. Counts: 34 VERIFIED; 4 OVERSTATED; 1 MISQUOTED; 1 NOT FOUND / unverifiable.** The NOT FOUND item is not judged false: it is an exact-diacritic claim whose cited online Hafez text has stripped emphatic marks.

1. **VERIFIED — §1 /p/.** Digest: “`/p/ ... substitutes to /b/`.” Hafez says /p/ “is normally replaced by its voiced counterpart /b/” and gives initial, medial, and final examples (`bântâloon`, `bâsboor`, `bajeb`) [ema1958 §14]. Galal says, despite OCR corruption, “Arabic has no /p/, the available alternative is [b]” and that this “works systematically” [kwpl-ot-cairene-loan pp.5–6].
2. **VERIFIED — §1 /v/.** Digest: “`/v/ ... substitutes to /f/ (also /b/, /w/)`.” Hafez explicitly lists /f/, /b/, and /w/ outcomes and retained prestige /v/: `fella`, `koberta`, `wâbuur`, `monâwrâ`, `viitu` [ema1958 §15].
3. **VERIFIED — §1 /ʒ/.** Digest: “`/ʒ/ ... substitutes to /ʃ/`.” Hafez says the voiced alveopalatal is replaced by its voiceless counterpart and gives `garaaǧ ~ garaaš`, `debrejaaǧ ~ debrejaaš` [ema1958 §17].
4. **VERIFIED — §1 and §3 /tʃ/.** Digest: “`/tʃ/ ... → /ʃ/, though increasingly retained`.” Hafez gives `šebs`, `wenš`, `marš`, `konšertu`, `šiili` [ema1958 §17]; Galal's PDF table prints retained final affricates in `kalatʃ` and `ʔisbiitʃ` [kwpl-ot-cairene-loan pp.2–3].
5. **VERIFIED — §1 and §3 /dʒ/.** Digest: “`/dʒ/ ... → /ʒ/`.” Hafez explicitly gives *Jeep* `/dʒiip/` → `/ʒebb/` [ema1958 §17].
6. **VERIFIED — §1 and §3 /ŋ/ retention, narrowly.** Digest: “source /ŋ/ simply retained: *spring* → `[ʔispiriŋ]`.” Broselow's table prints exactly that Cairene form [broselow-position-quality p.295]. The Hafez `ng` spellings add no independent phonetic analysis, as the digest correctly notes.
7. **VERIFIED — §2 syllable template.** Digest: “exactly one onset consonant ... zero to two coda consonants.” The held descriptions state one-and-only-one onset and final CC, with medial coda limited to one [broselow1976 pp.34, 48; ijllnet-cairene-english-syll pp.92–96; hassig2011-cairene p.31].
8. **VERIFIED — §2 integrated-register onset inventory is empty.** Digest quotes “Every ECA syllable must begin with one and only one consonant” and “no ECA words begin with consonant clusters.” Both occur in Broselow [broselow1976 pp.20, 48]. The digest also correctly records Abdel-Massih's rare-loan/register conflict.
9. **VERIFIED — §2 unrestricted final CC sonority.** Digest: “any combination of word-final consonants regardless of sonority.” Broselow says exactly this and cites `[ʔakl]` as the typological example [broselow-arabic-syll p.3].
10. **VERIFIED — §2 CCC ban.** Digest: “CCC is banned everywhere.” Broselow calls three-consonant sequences “anathema” and gives phrase-wide repair; Galal states that CCC is not allowed even when heterosyllabic [broselow1976 p.1; kwpl-ot-cairene-loan p.7].
11. **VERIFIED — §2 cross-word syllabification.** Digest: `katab # ilgawaab` → `[ka.ta.b# il.ga.waab]`. Broselow prints this example and states that Cairene syllabification operates across words [broselow-arabic-syll p.5; broselow1976 pp.35–36].
12. **VERIFIED — §3 obstruent+resonant anaptyxis.** Digest: “rising sonority ... vowel inserted inside the cluster.” Broselow states “within an onset cluster of rising sonority (an obstruent followed by a sonorant)” and prints `firiizar, bilastik, birinter, kirimbilin, silajd, siwetar` [broselow-position-quality p.295].
13. **VERIFIED — §3 /s/+stop prothesis.** Digest: “vowel inserted before the cluster, plus an inserted glottal stop.” Broselow contrasts the preceding forms with `ʔiskii, ʔistadi, ʔispiriŋ` and explicitly says prothesis requires an added glottal stop because Arabic bans vowel-initial syllables [broselow-position-quality pp.295, 297–298].
14. **OVERSTATED — §3 and §8 `/sr/` is assigned anaptyxis.** Digest: “`/sl sr/` ... anaptyxis.” The source attests `/sl/` and `/sw/`, not `/sr/`, and then warns that “some or all S-resonant clusters” may pattern with prothesis [broselow-position-quality pp.295, 308]. `/sr/` must be marked unattested/undecided alongside `/sm sn/`, not encoded from `silajd` and `siwetar` alone.
15. **OVERSTATED — §3 “Hafez independently confirms the mixed [sonority-conditioned] pattern.”** Hafez documents both pre-cluster and cluster-internal vowels, but states no sonority-conditioned division; indeed `classeur` has a prothetic/anaptyctic doublet [ema1958 §29]. Her data are compatible with Broselow's mixed pattern, but do not independently confirm its conditioning.
16. **VERIFIED — §3 three-member onset repair.** Digest: *street* → `/ʔistiriit/` with prothesis, glottal insertion, and anaptyxis. Broselow's footnote explicitly contrasts hypothetical one-vowel `[sit.riit]` with actual `[ʔis.ti.riit]` [broselow-position-quality p.298 n.5].
17. **VERIFIED — §3 medial/phrase CCC site C2–C3.** Digest: “`Ø → i / C C _ C`.” Broselow prints Cairene `ka.tab.ti.lu` versus Iraqi `ki.ta.bit.la` and describes the vowel as after C2 in Cairene [broselow-arabic-syll pp.8–9]. Galal's PDF table independently gives `bankinut, ʃarkiskiin, bustiman`, all after C2 [kwpl-ot-cairene-loan pp.2–3].
18. **VERIFIED — §3 default /i/.** Digest: “Default: /i/, realized [e].” Broselow's formal rule inserts `[+syll,+hi,-bk,-long]` [broselow1976 pp.2, 23]; her 2015 typology places Cairene in `TiRA, iSTA`, default /i/ in both positions [broselow-position-quality p.309 table 21].
19. **VERIFIED — §3 round-vowel exception.** Digest quotes “[u] may appear as the anaptyctic vowel when the next lexical vowel is round.” That is verbatim Broselow [broselow-position-quality p.295 n.4], and Galal's PDF table has `burujiktur, fulurusint, furuut, guruub` [kwpl-ot-cairene-loan p.3].
20. **VERIFIED — §3 harmony blocking.** Digest: harmony is blocked in prothetic /s/+obstruent and compound-internal C2–C3 sites. Galal's analysis says the default is [i] where an s+obstruent intervenes or where an epenthetic vowel intervenes across the compound cluster; his data are `ʔistaf/ʔistuk/ʔistub` and `bankinut/ʃarkiskiin/bustiman` [kwpl-ot-cairene-loan pp.13–17].
21. **VERIFIED — §3 epenthesis over deletion.** Digest quotes “CA seems to show a preference for epenthesis over deletion.” Galal states this and concludes that Cairene “opts for epenthesis rather than deletion” in the loan data [kwpl-ot-cairene-loan pp.4, 17–18].
22. **OVERSTATED — §3.8 and §4 donor length.** Digest: “Donor vowel LENGTH is not inherited. Length is assigned by Cairene stress,” later strengthened to “an Irish long vowel survives only if” Cairene stress licenses it. Hafez only says vowels are “especially lengthened when they are in a stressed syllable” and that stress follows EA rather than source-language patterns [ema1958 §26]. She does not establish categorical erasure of donor quantity, and every `source_ipa` cell is blank (§7), so the corpus cannot test preservation versus loss. Native unstressed/closed-syllable shortening is real, but the initial donor-length mapping remains unsettled.
23. **VERIFIED — §3 closed-syllable shortening.** Digest: `Vː → V / _CC`. Broselow gives the shortening rule and mid-vowel raising upon shortening [broselow1976 pp.18–19]; Watson illustrates Cairene `/baːb+ha/ → [babha]` and `/kaːtib+a/ → [katba]` [watson2007-syllabification p.340].
24. **VERIFIED — §4 three-step stress rule.** Watson's (29) is exactly: (a) stress final CVV(C) or CVCC; (b) otherwise antepenult if penult and antepenult are light unless pre-antepenult is light; (c) otherwise penult [watson2011-word-stress p.3003]. McCarthy's (2) is substantively identical [mccarthy1979-stress-syll p.446].
25. **VERIFIED — §4 `madˈrasa`.** Watson states: “In words with a heavy antepenult, Cairene stresses the light penult,” contrasting Cairene `[madˈrasa]` with Beirut/Damascene `[ˈmadrase]` [watson2011-word-stress p.2991]. The digest's warning is correct.
26. **OVERSTATED — §4 long runs of light syllables.** Digest presents Langendoen's parity rule as the fuller Cairene rule “for runs of four or more light syllables.” Watson immediately says such long strings are “unattested in Cairene”; the evidence is Classical Arabic pronunciation taught in Cairo [watson2011-word-stress pp.3003–3004]. The rule is reasonable for generated long forms, but its evidential domain must be stated.
27. **VERIFIED — §4 17-row worked table.** All 17 outcomes agree with their cited sources and the stated step: `kaˈtabt, ʔaˈbe(h), sakaˈkiːn, tˤalaˈbaːt, ˈʔabadan, muxˈtalifa, kataˈbitu, jikˈtibu, ʕaˈmalti, marˈtaba, ˈbeːtak` are Watson (29), with overlapping McCarthy examples; `madˈrasa` and `binˈtina` are Watson pp.2991–2992; `ˈkatab, ˈkatabit, ˈkatba, makˈtaba` match the Wikipedia stress section. No worked-row error found.
28. **VERIFIED — §4 epenthetic vowels count for stress.** Watson says a penultimate post-CVC epenthetic vowel is stressed like any other and compares `[binˈtina]`, `[madˈrasa]`, `[fihˈmitu]` [watson2011-word-stress p.2992].
29. **VERIFIED — §8 underlying emphatics.** Digest: `/tˤ dˤ sˤ zˤ rˤ lˤ/`, with /q/ exceptional. Broselow says only those six segments are underlyingly emphatic [broselow1976 p.xiii] and “All segments except /q/ occur both emphatic and non-emphatic” [p.32].
30. **VERIFIED — §8 syllable-domain Emp-Sp.** Digest: the whole syllable becomes emphatic. Broselow states “The minimum domain of emphasis is the sequence CV” and formulates Emp-Sp as the same-syllable rule [broselow1976 pp.32, 41].
31. **VERIFIED — §8 RES.** Digest: right spread requires a consonant-final emphatic syllable and a following low-vowel syllable. Broselow first identifies low-vowel conditioning, then revises RES because `ħarraana` and `latˤiifa` have open preceding syllables [broselow1976 pp.43–45].
32. **VERIFIED — §8 LES.** Digest: leftward spread reaches the word edge and is word-boundary-sensitive, with variable prefix/cross-word behavior. Broselow says exactly this, notes variable inflectional prefixes, and treats the radio-Arabic cross-word example as style/formality dependent [broselow1976 pp.46–47].
33. **NOT FOUND / UNVERIFIABLE, not contradicted — §8 exact Hafez emphatic transcriptions.** Digest assigns emphatics to the listed `tânt, bâttârejjâ, moodâ, sâloon, vââzâ, qomisjoon...` forms. Hafez's prose does say alveolars become emphatic beside back vowels and names these examples [ema1958 §§19–20], so the generalization is supported. But the held online transcription has stripped the emphatic diacritics; the exact segment-by-segment emphatic markings cannot be checked there. Absence of a mark is not evidence for a plain consonant.
34. **VERIFIED — §8 three independent “loan-emphasis” cases.** The PDF pages, not OCR, print `lukanḍa` ‘hotel’ [abdelmassih-v4 p.147], `ṣaloon` ‘train compartment’ and `tiṛaam ~ tiṛamwaay` ‘tramway’ [p.150]. The subscript dots are visible. The digest also correctly retracts the former `braavo` claim: Abdel-Massih p.26 prints plain `/braavo/`.
35. **MISQUOTED — §1 and §8 marginal /mˤ/ evidence.** Digest: “Broselow reports only `mayya` ... as an /mˤ/ candidate.” Broselow's footnote says “There are three possible exceptions”: emphatic `mayya`, `hamma` ‘boil’ vs plain `hamma` ‘he bathed’, and emphatic `ʕumʔ` ‘depth’ vs plain `ʕunʔ` ‘neck’ [broselow1976 p.57 n.8]. `mayya` is disputed by Abdel-Massih, but it is not Broselow's only candidate datum.
36. **VERIFIED — §7 Khalifa status.** Digest says the eleven Khalifa rows are predictions, not observations. The PDF repeatedly uses starred forms under tables headed “Error,” says learners “tend to” produce them, and for codas says “it is expected that” learners will produce `strengith` and `tempits` [ijllnet-cairene-english-syll pp.108–110].
37. **VERIFIED — §6 article assimilation.** Digest gives obligatory assimilation before dentals plus /ʃ/, and optional /k g/. Abdel-Massih says /l/ is obligatorily assimilated to a following dental or /ʃ/ and gives `ilkalb ~ ikkalb` (and the corresponding /g/ pair) [abdelmassih-v3 pp.83–84].
38. **VERIFIED — §6 nisba attachment.** Digest quotes “The termination -V or -(Vy)V is deleted before suffixing /-i/.” This sentence and the `masr → masri`, `madrasa → madrasi`, `turkiyya → turki` examples occur in Abdel-Massih [abdelmassih-v3 pp.142–143].
39. **VERIFIED — §6 vowel-final loan suffix avoidance.** Digest: `kiilu, radyu, banyu, antinna` “seldom take pronominal suffixes” and use `bitaaʕ`. Abdel-Massih states exactly this [abdelmassih-v3 p.18].
40. **VERIFIED — §5 source status of popular romanization.** Digest says Help:IPA's examples use forms commonly used in Egypt. The page states: “The romanization of the examples is the commonly used form in Egypt” [wiki-help-ipa-egy, opening note]. The digest's final recommended spelling is a project design, not a source claim.

## 2. Uncited or under-cited claims

- **`/g/ → g normally` (§3.6)** is sensible from the native inventory, but the table cites only the exceptional spelling-mediated `/g/ → ʁ~ɣ/` set. Add an identity-loan example or label `/g/ → g` as inventory-based fallback.
- **`/sr/ → sir-` (§8.5)** is neither cited nor tagged unattested. The cited forms instantiate `/sl sw/`, and Broselow expressly leaves S-resonant behavior partly open. Mark `/sr/` undecided/unattested.
- **`/ʒ/ ... could reasonably assimilate` to the article (§6.1)** is a design inference. It needs `(unattested)`; the Cairene source classifies /ʃ/, not loan /ʒ/.
- **Loan etymologies for `lukanḍa`, `ṣaloon`, `tiṛamwaay` (§8.1)** are presented as `< Ital./Fr./Eng.` while §7 correctly says Abdel-Massih states no etymologies. Mark the donor identifications inferred or cite an etymological source.
- **“Before a /ʔ/-initial stem: `il-`, e.g. `ilʔarnab`” (§6.1)** has no local citation. It should point to the Abdel-Massih article paradigm/page.
- **The recommended popular romanization (§5.2)** is mostly a declared design, which is fine, but the claim that `ou` “reads correctly to an English eye” is not evidence-backed. Keep it explicitly as a project choice rather than an Egyptian fact.

## 3. Rule/example consistency

### §3 rules lacking a proper instantiation

- **Donor `/ð/ → z` (learned) or `/d/` (inherited)** has only inherited/learned Arabic historical examples in the prose and no non-Arabic loan row in `attested.csv`. It should not masquerade as a demonstrated foreign-loan mapping.
- **Donor `/q/ → ʔ ~ k`** has no `attested.csv` row.
- **`/g/ → g normally`** has no explicitly cited source→target identity example in the substitution row; only exceptional `/g/ → ɣ~ʁ` is instantiated.
- **Three-member donor coda repair** is instantiated only by Khalifa's predicted L2 forms `strengith, tempits`, not observed Cairene loans. The digest flags this correctly, but a rule file must keep it at prediction-level confidence.
- All central epenthesis rules do have real lexical-loan instantiations: internal onset epenthesis (`firizar`, `bilastik`), /s/+stop prothesis (`ʔiskii`, `ʔistadi`, `ʔistub`), combined sCC repair (`ʔistiriit` in Broselow 2015), and medial C2–C3 repair (`bankinut`, `ʃarkiskiin`, `bustiman`).

## 4. Internal contradictions and missing CONFLICT lines

1. **Direct contradiction: final devoicing.** §1 says Abdel-Massih reports utterance-final devoicing of `/r l/` after voiceless obstruents. §3.7 then says “No final devoicing is reported by any source.” The intended claim is evidently **no final obstruent devoicing**. Rewrite it that way; otherwise §3.7 contradicts §1 and §8.3.
2. **Direct contradiction: `braavo`.** Digest §8 correctly says Abdel-Massih prints plain `braavo`; `attested.csv` row 297 says emphatic `bṛaavo`. Fix the TSV row.
3. **Evidence conflict hidden by categorical wording: donor length.** §3.8/§4 claim donor length is erased, while §7 says donor pronunciation is absent in every row. The held data can establish Cairene stress assignment and native shortening, not categorical source-quantity noninheritance. This needs an explicit `not covered`/open-question line.
4. **Target-inventory decision not surfaced as a CONFLICT: `/ŋ/`.** §1 calls it nonphonemic/allophonic, while §3.6 and §8.3 recommend retaining loan `[ŋ]`. Broselow provides one printed retained loan, but the digest never decides whether the rule file's output inventory admits marginal `/ŋ/` or rewrites it to an `/n/`+/g/ sequence. Record this as a register/inventory conflict like `/p v ʒ tʃ/`.
5. **Initial-cluster generalization is broader than its own open questions.** §3.1 announces a complete sonority-conditioned algorithm; §8.5/§9 later admit `/sm sn sr kn gn mn/` are unresolved. The headline rule should be narrowed to attested classes: obstruent+liquid/glide examples versus /s/+stop, with the remaining classes explicitly outside its domain.
6. **`autobus` transcription levels need reconciliation.** §3.7 says initial glottal insertion is obligatory; Hafez/Broselow give `ʔotobiis`, while Abdel-Massih's lexicon prints `utubiis` (attested row 315). This may be predictable late insertion omitted in the lexicon's representation, but the digest should state that rather than leave two output forms unexplained.
7. The digest appropriately records genuine source conflicts on initial CC, short `/e o/`, construct `-it/-et`, and coarse versus conditioned emphasis spread. No missing conflict was found in the full three-step stress algorithm or its worked examples.

## 5. Fitness for purpose

- **Do not encode the current donor-length statement.** A deterministic Irish adapter still lacks the initial mapping from Irish long/short quantity to Cairene underlying quantity. The sources only determine what happens *after* quantity is represented (stress, unstressed shortening, closed-syllable shortening, one-long-vowel effects).
- **Narrow the onset repair dispatch table.** Safe classes are `/s/+stop → ʔisC-`, obstruent+liquid/glide → internal vowel, and sCC like *street* → both repairs. `/sr sm sn kn gn mn/` need explicit unresolved branches; otherwise the rule writer will silently generalize beyond the data.
- **Choose one output register.** `/p v ʒ tʃ/`, initial CC, and likely `/ŋ/` vary with integration/prestige. The digest describes the dial well but does not select the deterministic point needed by the tool.
- **Keep predicted L2 forms out of training/verification counts.** The eleven Khalifa rows are useful hypotheses, not attestations. A parser cannot reliably infer that from prose notes. Two Makkan rows must be removed outright from the Egyptian dataset.
- **Correct the emphasis evidence before broad→emphatic design work.** Add Broselow's other possible `/mˤ/` examples; correct `braavo`; retain the three PDF-verified loan-emphasis cases. Exact Hafez diacritics remain unavailable, so do not use absence of emphasis in Hafez-derived TSV IPA as negative evidence.
- **Specify the `/ŋ/` output representation.** “Retain /ŋ/” is supported by one Broselow transcription, but conflicts with the core inventory. Decide whether it is a marginal admitted segment or an orthographic `ng` sequence.
- **For long generated forms, label the parity stress rule's status.** It is based on Cairene pronunciation of Classical Arabic because long all-light strings are unattested in native Cairene. It is still the best available extension, but should not be presented as directly observed native lexical phonotactics.
- **Parser hygiene:** strip slash delimiters from 28 Abdel-Massih IPA cells, and make observed/predicted/comparison status machine-readable before using `attested.csv` as tests.

## 6. Verdict

### Required fixes before use

1. Retract or qualify the categorical claim that donor vowel length is not inherited; mark Irish quantity mapping unresolved.
2. Correct `attested.csv` row 297 from emphatic `bṛaavo` to plain `braavo`, remove the two Makkan rows, and segregate/filter the eleven Khalifa predictions.
3. Change “no final devoicing” to “no final **obstruent** devoicing.”
4. Restrict the onset algorithm to attested cluster classes; mark `/sr/` (as well as `/sm sn kn gn mn/`) unresolved.
5. Correct the `/mˤ/` summary: Broselow gives three possible exception sets, not only `mayya`.
6. Resolve or explicitly expose the marginal `/ŋ/` inventory decision.
7. Strip slash delimiters from Abdel-Massih `target_ipa` fields before machine use.

### Suggestions

- State that the long-light-string stress extension comes from Cairene pronunciation of Classical Arabic, not native Cairene words.
- Mark inferred etymologies and the proposed article assimilation of loan `/ʒ/` as inferences/unattested.
- Re-provenance or remove the general-Arabic duplicate `ʔotobiis` row.
- Keep the three PDF-verified loan-emphasis cases as the reliable segment-marked evidence; treat Hafez's stripped exact diacritics as unverifiable rather than plain.
