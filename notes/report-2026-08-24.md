## Things that changed under us

- **Belgian Dutch** is now a positive choice: PHOIBLE row 2169's own source is Verhoeven 2005
  (the Belgian JIPA illustration), and Belgian keeps the /x/–/ɣ/ contrast underlyingly (the
  phonetics are mostly voiceless), which suits Irish /ɣ/. Cairene confirmed; its article is
  **/il-/**, not *al-*.
- **Welsh is less settled**: PHOIBLE row 2406 is from Llanwrtyd, in Powys, and one source groups
  the traditional Powys dialect with the North. The inventory is Southern (no /ɨ/); whether the
  phonotactic/stress detail is, no held source can say.
- **Your example transcriptions.** *Ciara* /ˈkɪə.ɾˠə/ has a broad /k/; spelling and *ciall*
  /ciəl̪ˠ/ predict slender /c/. Flagged, not corrected. *Matánach* is fine everywhere (an earlier
  note that it hits a Dutch constraint was wrong — the /x/ follows the schwa of *-ach*).
- **Cairene stress**: the first draft had the signature rule backwards (Cairene stresses the
  light penult after a heavy antepenult: *madˈrasa*). Caught in review and re-derived from
  Watson; worth your own second look since it touches every output.
- **Georgian repairs nothing in the held data**: no epenthesis, deletion or prothesis in any loan
  source; a four-consonant medial cluster imported intact. A preservation whitelist, not a
  proven no-repair grammar. The only Irish onset needing its own decision is /sn/ (low stakes);
  /st/ falls out of the /p t k/ series choice below.

## Decisions that are yours (highest impact first)

1. **Broad/slender, per target.** No target has a loan precedent for a palatalization contrast.
   Two things from the Irish side frame every option: articulatorily the contrast is *most*
   robust on labials (also the most variable, having no lingual anchor) and *weakest* on
   coronals (where it is largely a sibilant-quality difference, /sˠ/ vs /ʃ/); and Irish has no
   coda neutralization — any "drop it in codas" rule is a design simplification, not a
   transfer. The Irish digest's steer (its option 5, "the least destructive ordering" — a
   recommendation, not an Irish rule): keep the contrast on coronals as /s/~/ʃ/ and /t/~/tʃ/
   in all positions, drop it on labials and dorsals. Per target (each §8.1 tabulates options):
   - **Welsh**: ⟨si⟩ already means both /ʃ/ and /sj/, and Southern Welsh palatalizes /s/ from a
     preceding front vowel (*mis* [miːʃ]) — the nearest native thing to a slender-coda effect;
     but Welsh never synthesizes palatalization from a /Cj/ input in loans.
   - **Dutch**: Cʲ → Cj has onset support (*Pjotr, Tjeerd*) but /j/ is barred from most codas,
     so that option collapses codas; the vowel-colouring option doesn't. Dutch ranks this as
     the #1 blocker — nothing downstream can be finalized before it.
   - **Georgian**: broad → Cʷ via Georgian's own labialization slot is a real Georgian mechanism
     (unattested in loans); slender → Ci has only editorial, Russian-mediated attestation.
   - **Arabic**: broad → emphatic rests on Hafez's rule [alveolar] → [emphatic] / _[back V]
     (~15 examples) plus three corroborating cases. Two caveats: **emphasis spread runs to the
     left word edge and will flatten the contrast again** unless you choose not to implement
     spread; and half the broad series (/p f k g ŋ x ɣ w n/) has no emphatic counterpart.
2. **Does the generator emit mutated/inflected Irish forms?** In citation form, the
   mutation-initial onsets (/w x ɣ ç j h ŋ ɲ/ word-initially) never reach a target. With
   vocatives, genitive chains or article phrases, every target needs rules for them. This
   sizes every rule file.
3. **Project-wide romanization convention.** Authentic target orthography plus a pronunciation
   key, or a separate English-reader respelling? Dutch orthography alone fails the
   English-reader requirement; Welsh is near-phonemic but single ⟨f⟩ = /v/ (plus ⟨dd⟩, ⟨c⟩, w/y); Georgian is forced by
   the existing names (below). One decision for all five output files.
4. **Strand 4 overlays on Georgian.** The digest reduces "overlay vs. pure Georgian" to nine
   explicit overlays with evidential status (georgian §9 table). The consequential ones:
   - **Bare-stem output** (no nominative *-i*), which is what makes consonant-final names
     possible. Overlay, not rule: Georgian stems end in any consonant but /h/, yet the attested
     stem-final clusters are word-*medial* in real words, and no source explains why native
     C-final names (*Davit, Tamar*) escape *-i*.
   - **Ejective vs. aspirate for Irish /p t k/** — both attested; the Geo-like layer is
     ejective (the digest recommends it); the choice is word-level (harmonic within a word).
   - **/w/ → /u/ or /v/** — unattested either way, and it changes nearly every lenited-b/m name.
   - **Syncope** — Georgian's native cluster *creation* (a non-high vowel before a stem-final
     sonorant drops when a -V(C) suffix is added: *megobari* → *megobrebi*) is a grammar lever
     that delivers the harsh-cluster aesthetic without an overlay.
   - **Romanization**: national 2002 (apostrophe *after* the ejective) plus `x`, `tch`, `y`=/i/.
     None of the five existing names parses under the national system; *Kas'queil* must become
     *Kasq'ueil*, and *Th'tysh* fails on three counts with no fix offered.
   - "Devoice everything / drop nasals" (the June feature-filter idea) has no Georgian basis;
     the digest did not evaluate it.
5. **Welsh**: (a) ordering of Irish vowel length vs. Welsh penultimate stress/length — the
   length-first ordering makes many outputs *final*-stressed, which decides whether names sound
   Welsh; (b) initial *l-/r-* fortition to /ɬ/, /r̥/ (*Lasair-* → *Llasair-*?) — Parry-Williams
   states it, many loans ignore it; (c) whether Irish /sC-/ gets prothetic *y-* (stated, rarely
   pronounced); (d) which Southern variety to target (welsh §9.6).
6. **Dutch**: adaptation register (etymological / nativized / spelling-pronunciation — Flemish
   speakers prefer the first for French, the third otherwise); stress precedence (Irish vs.
   Dutch tendencies); and the repair where a long vowel directly precedes a voiceless fricative
   (*bách*) — three legal outputs, none chosen.
7. **Strand 5 (Old Irish)** has no derivation path in the sources (Proto-Celtic→Old Irish only).
   Recommended: eDIL lookup + inflection by stem class — a lookup task, not a rule task.
